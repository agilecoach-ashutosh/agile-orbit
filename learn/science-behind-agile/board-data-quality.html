METRICS & DECISION MAKING

A Stale Board
Is a Broken Sensor

Why accurate Jira and GitLab data matters: not because the tool needs updating, but because teams make decisions from the picture of reality recorded inside it.

Reality→Board Data→Metrics→Decision
THE IDEA

Your delivery board is a measurement system.

If the work and the board disagree, every downstream view can become misleading: WIP, cycle time, aging, blockers, throughput, forecasts and status reporting.

The goal is not “keep Jira updated.” The goal is to keep the shared model of work aligned with reality.
CASE STUDY → PRACTICE

From bad infrastructure data to bad delivery data

01 · THE CONSEQUENCE OF BAD DATA

French train case study

Lack of proper Data Quality controls

In 2014

French train operator (SNCF) ordered 2,000 new trains for their lines in Paris and throughout France.

The problem

Unfortunately, the data that they were using in the project was not accurate.

What was missing

The national rail operator (RFF) had sent SNCF the dimensions of stations built less than 30 years ago – however, stations built more than 50 years ago were several centimeters smaller. As a result the new trains were too big for the older stations.

The consequence

The financial consequences of using data of bad quality were significant - the mistake sparked an urgent $68m operation to modify 1,300 platforms on the regional network.

The engineering decision was only as good as the data describing the system.
02 · NOW BRING IT TO DELIVERY

Jira or GitLab is also a model of reality.

When the model is stale, the team sees a system that does not actually exist.

Actual workTestingbut board saysDevelopment
Actual workBlockedbut board saysIn Progress
Actual workFinishedbut board saysIn Progress
Actual workStartedbut board saysTo Do
A board that does not reflect reality is not transparency. It is misinformation.
03 · THE DOMINO EFFECT

What breaks when the board is stale?

A mathematically perfect dashboard can still be wrong when the source data is wrong.

Wrong status→Wrong WIP
Wrong start / finish dates→Wrong cycle time & aging
Work not closed→Wrong throughput
Missing blockers→Hidden waiting & risk
Missing dependencies→False planning confidence
Bad historical data→Weak forecasts
Bad Data→Bad Metrics→Bad Decisions
04 · DON'T UPDATE THE TOOL FOR THE TOOL

Maintain only the data that helps the team see and decide.

Automate what the system can infer. Ask people to maintain what still requires human context.

HUMANS SHOULD MAKE VISIBLE
  • Work actually started? Reflect the active state.
  • Blocked? Make the blocker visible.
  • Dependency changed? Link or record it.
  • Priority/context changed? Reflect the new decision.
AUTOMATE WHERE PRACTICAL
  • Merge / pull-request activity
  • CI pipeline and deployment state
  • Workflow timestamps
  • Release and environment signals
The purpose is trustworthy shared visibility—not administrative perfection.
05 · MAKE IT A TEAM HABIT

Our Board = Our Shared Reality

Before a Daily Scrum, flow review or delivery conversation, ask one question:

“Does this board represent what is actually happening right now?”
Started?Move it.
Finished?Close it.
Blocked?Show it.
Dependency changed?Reflect it.
If the board and reality disagree, trust reality — then fix the board.
THE POINT

Don't maintain the tool for the tool.

Maintain a trustworthy representation of work so the team can coordinate, expose flow problems and make better decisions from real evidence.
