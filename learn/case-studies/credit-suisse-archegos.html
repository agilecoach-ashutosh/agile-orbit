Credit Suisse & Archegos: when risk signals exist but nobody stops the system

Archegos was not a case where risk was invisible. Limits were exceeded, exposure was unusually concentrated, and warnings existed. The failure was turning those signals into timely action while revenue and client pressure pushed in the opposite direction.

Live case2021BankingCounterparty risk9–11 min
Economic impactCredit Suisse suffered approximately $5.5B in losses after Archegos defaulted.
Risk concentrationFINMA said Credit Suisse's Archegos position reached about $24B in March 2021.
System failureRisk limits were repeatedly exceeded, yet exposure was not reduced fast enough.
01 · THE SITUATION

A profitable client became a concentrated system risk

Archegos used highly leveraged synthetic positions with several investment banks.

Credit Suisse's relationship with Archegos grew into an exposure far larger than a normal client relationship. FINMA later found that the bank's position linked to Archegos was about $24 billion in March 2021, four times the position of the next-largest hedge-fund client and more than half of Credit Suisse Group's equity.

The uncomfortable part is that risk monitoring was already showing limit overruns. But instead of consistently forcing exposure down, limits were sometimes increased and collateral demands remained insufficient. When Archegos could no longer meet margin calls, concentrated positions had to be unwound into a falling market.

Revenue pressureA large client relationship can make risk reduction commercially painful.
Limit overrunsRepeated exceptions can normalize abnormal risk until the exception becomes the operating model.
ConcentrationCollateral may fail as protection when the underlying positions are highly correlated and must be sold together.
EscalationSignals only protect the system when decision-makers have authority and incentives to act on them.
02 · DECISION POINT

A valuable client keeps breaching risk limits. What do you do?

The relationship is profitable. Exposure is rising. Risk teams are concerned. Reducing the position could damage revenue and the client relationship.

Choose the action you would defend to revenue leaders, risk leaders and the board.

The system behaved much closer to A than C.

FINMA found repeated limit overruns, insufficient objections and repeated increases to limits. The exposure remained exceptionally concentrated. When Archegos collapsed in March 2021, Credit Suisse suffered the largest loss among affected banks—about $5.5 billion.

03 · WHAT FAILED

The warning system was not the same as a control system

Exceptions became normalRepeated limit breaches were not converted into decisive exposure reduction.
Client preference competed with risk disciplineFINMA found responsible employees acted in favour of the client even as risk grew.
Concentration weakened the hedgeLarge positions in a small number of issuers meant liquidation itself amplified losses.
Senior visibility was insufficientFINMA found responsible executive-board members were not adequately engaged despite the scale of exposure.
04 · OUTCOME & TRADE-OFF

Approximately $5.5B was lost after warnings failed to become action

The Federal Reserve later said Credit Suisse suffered approximately $5.5 billion in losses because of the Archegos default and had failed to adequately manage the counterparty risk despite repeated warnings. FINMA concluded that Credit Suisse had seriously and systematically violated financial-market law in the relationship.

The useful organizational lesson is broader than banking: a dashboard can be accurate and still fail if the organization rewards delay, tolerates exceptions or lacks a clear escalation path.

05 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

Transparency without response is theatre

Escalation designSignals need explicit thresholds that trigger action, not merely discussion.
Incentive conflictRevenue incentives can overpower control incentives unless governance makes the trade-off explicit.
Exception debtEvery tolerated exception makes the next exception easier to justify.
Concentration riskLocally rational growth can create system-level fragility when dependencies become too concentrated.
06 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions for teams where warnings keep repeating

SignalWhich warning has appeared more than once without changing behaviour?
ThresholdWhat evidence should automatically trigger a stop, escalation or scope reduction?
IncentiveWho benefits from accepting the risk, and who carries the downside?
AuthorityCan the people who see the risk actually stop the work?
ExceptionWhich temporary exception has quietly become normal operating practice?
