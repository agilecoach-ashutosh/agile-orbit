Citi: when the transformation itself must prove it is working

In 2020 U.S. regulators required Citi to correct longstanding deficiencies in risk management, data governance and internal controls. Four years later regulators said progress remained insufficient in important areas and imposed additional penalties. The learning question is how a large transformation proves sustainable change rather than activity.

01 · THE SITUATION

Longstanding weaknesses became a transformation mandate

Regulators did not ask for a new slogan. They required measurable correction of underlying systems.

In October 2020, the OCC assessed a $400 million penalty against Citibank and required broad corrective actions related to enterprise risk management, compliance risk management, data governance and internal controls. The Federal Reserve issued a related enforcement action against Citigroup.

This created a difficult transformation problem: how do you modernize a huge institution while simultaneously proving to regulators that the change is sufficient, sustainable and embedded in day-to-day operations?

ScaleData and controls span businesses, geographies, systems and regulatory obligations.
MilestonesTransformation plans can look green while underlying capability remains immature.
Legacy complexityOld data models and systems make remediation slower and more interdependent.
Regulatory evidenceProgress must be demonstrated through outcomes and controls, not only program reporting.
02 · DECISION POINT

Your transformation has hundreds of workstreams. Regulators say progress is still not sustainable. What changes?

Choose what would convince you that the system is genuinely improving, not merely reporting progress.

Regulators pushed toward the logic of B.

In 2024 the OCC amended its 2020 order because Citi had failed to meet remediation milestones and make sufficient and sustainable progress, and required the bank to prioritize remediation and allocate sufficient resources. The Federal Reserve separately cited insufficient progress in data-quality management and missing compensating controls.

03 · TRANSFORMATION TRAP

Activity can hide the absence of durable capability

Milestones are proxiesCompleting a program milestone does not prove that everyday risk decisions now work differently.
Data quality is systemicBad data propagates into regulatory reports, decisions, controls and customer outcomes.
Compensating controls matterIf the permanent solution is late, temporary controls must reduce the active risk rather than merely document it.
Resources reveal priorityRegulators explicitly required sufficient resources—an important signal that priority is visible in capacity allocation, not speeches.
04 · OUTCOME

$400M in 2020, then another ~$135.6M in 2024

The OCC's 2020 penalty was $400 million. In July 2024, the OCC imposed another $75 million and the Federal Reserve imposed $60.6 million, for approximately $135.6 million in additional penalties announced that day.

The stronger lesson is not “transformation failed.” Large remediation programs can make real progress while still falling short of the required standard. The core question is whether capability improvement is measurable, sustainable and fast enough relative to the risk.

05 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

Transform outcomes, not only structures

Outcome metricsMeasure control effectiveness, data quality and decision reliability—not only task completion.
WIP managementHundreds of simultaneous remediation initiatives can dilute scarce expertise and leadership attention.
Evidence-based governanceIndependent evidence should challenge optimistic program narratives.
Sustainable changeThe new way of operating must survive beyond the transformation program and its dedicated teams.
06 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions for large transformation portfolios

OutcomeWhich observable business behaviour proves this workstream has actually improved the system?
WIPHow many transformation initiatives compete for the same scarce experts?
EvidenceWhat independent data could disprove our green status?
ControlWhat temporary safeguard exists while the long-term fix is unfinished?
DurabilityWould the improvement survive if the transformation office disappeared tomorrow?
