Intralinks: doing Scrum can still produce very little agility

Intralinks' first attempt at Scrum was described as well-intentioned and mechanical: teams adopted the visible framework but did not achieve the agility they wanted. The reboot focused more deeply on inspection, adaptation and the Scrum Values—making this a useful case about the difference between process adoption and an empirical working environment.

01 · THE SITUATION

Perfect ceremonies can hide a weak learning system

The danger of framework adoption is that visible compliance is much easier to measure than improved decision-making.

Scrum.org characterises Intralinks' first attempt as a “mechanical” implementation of Scrum that was carried out with good intentions and considerable effort, but failed to deliver the desired agility. That distinction matters: the problem was not laziness or refusal to adopt the framework. The team could perform the mechanics while still missing the deeper conditions that make empirical work useful.

The later reboot placed greater emphasis on inspection and adaptation and on the Scrum Values—Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect and Courage. Those values are not decorative culture statements. They change whether inconvenient evidence can be surfaced, whether teams can challenge plans, and whether adaptation is socially safe.

Ceremony complianceEvents can happen on schedule while difficult evidence is filtered or ignored.
Role performancePeople can occupy Scrum accountabilities without having enough authority, trust or clarity to fulfil them.
Low psychological safetyTeams that cannot openly expose uncertainty will produce polished status rather than useful transparency.
Adaptation gapInspection is wasted if plans, priorities or working practices cannot change afterward.
02 · DECISION POINT

Your Scrum implementation looks correct—but outcomes are not improving. What do you do?

Events happen. Roles exist. Boards are updated. Yet teams still feel slow and constrained.

Choose the response that targets the system beneath the ceremonies.

The Intralinks reboot aligned closest to Option C.

Scrum.org describes the second attempt as augmenting the mechanics with the fundamental ideas of inspection and adaptation and with the Scrum Values. The case is useful precisely because the same framework can produce very different outcomes depending on whether transparency is meaningful and adaptation is real.

03 · WHAT CHANGED

The reboot shifted attention from “doing Scrum” to learning through Scrum

Values became operationalOpenness and courage matter when teams need to surface bad news; respect and commitment matter when adapting together.
Inspection needed evidenceEvents became useful only when they exposed product, flow and team reality rather than rehearsed status.
Adaptation became the testThe important question after a Sprint event is not “did we hold it?” but “what changed because we learned something?”
Trust became infrastructureEmpiricism depends on people being able to show uncertainty and failure without immediately protecting themselves.
Mechanics regained purposeEvents, artifacts and accountabilities were treated as means to transparency, inspection and adaptation—not ends in themselves.
Coaching moved below the surfaceThe transformation problem became behavioural and systemic, not just procedural.
COMMON MISREAD“If teams follow the Scrum Guide closely enough, agility will follow automatically.”
VS
MORE USEFUL LESSONScrum creates opportunities for empiricism. Whether those opportunities produce learning depends on transparency, trust, values and the ability to adapt the system.
04 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

How to detect Cargo Cult Scrum

Events without decisionsIf Reviews and Retrospectives rarely change priorities or working agreements, inspection is ceremonial.
Transparency without safetyA board can be visible while reality remains hidden. Metrics and artifacts do not create honesty by themselves.
Roles without authorityA Product Owner who cannot influence product decisions is a proxy; a Scrum Master treated as meeting admin cannot improve the system.
Framework as diagnosticInstead of asking “Are we compliant?”, ask which Scrum element is exposing a constraint the organisation would rather ignore.
05 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions when Scrum looks busy but feels stuck

EvidenceWhat uncomfortable information is everyone aware of but not surfacing in Scrum events?
AdaptWhat was the last meaningful decision that changed because of a Sprint Review or Retrospective?
ValuesWhich Scrum Value is hardest to practice in the current organisational environment—and what makes it risky?
SystemWhich constraint is currently being blamed on “team maturity” even though the team cannot control it?
