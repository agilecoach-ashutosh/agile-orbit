TRANSFORM AT SCALE · BANKING

ING: changing the operating model, not just the ceremonies

ING Netherlands is often remembered for squads and tribes. The more useful case is about why a bank facing rapidly changing digital customer behaviour decided that fewer handoffs, multidisciplinary ownership and different governance mattered more than simply “making IT Agile.”

01 · THE SITUATION

A successful bank facing a different kind of competitor

The problem was not simply “our teams are too slow.” Customer expectations were changing outside traditional banking boundaries.

By the mid-2010s, ING Netherlands was operating in an environment where mobile and digital experiences were reshaping how customers judged service. Customers increasingly expected a journey to feel seamless whether it started in a branch, continued online or finished through another channel.

ING's leaders described agility as a way to adapt and steer faster, minimize handovers and bureaucracy, and empower multidisciplinary teams. That matters because it shifts the problem from “install a delivery method” to “redesign the system through which customer value moves.”

Functional specializationExpertise was organized into traditional structures that could create coordination boundaries.
Many handoffsA customer outcome could require decisions across multiple functions and channels.
Digital expectationsCustomers compared banking experiences with leading digital services, not only other banks.
Regulated realityRisk, legal, finance and other independent functions could not simply be absorbed into every squad.
02 · DECISION POINT

You're leading the transformation. How far do you change the system?

Assume the bank is performing reasonably well today, but digital customer expectations are accelerating. A radical reorganization carries execution, people and regulatory risk. Choose the path you would defend before revealing what ING did.

There is no perfect option. Choose the path you would defend.

ING moved closest to Option C.

ING Netherlands reorganized much of its headquarters around small multidisciplinary squads grouped into tribes, with chapters maintaining professional expertise. The design was inspired by technology companies, but ING adapted the model to banking and retained different arrangements where independence or operational context required it.

03 · WHAT CHANGED

The visible structure was only one part of the transformation

The famous labels matter less than the operating principles behind them.

Squads around client objectivesSmall, self-steering multidisciplinary teams were organized around missions rather than a sequence of functional handoffs.
Tribes for related missionsSquads with interconnected work were grouped so priorities, dependencies and shared context could be coordinated.
Chapters for craft and growthProfessional expertise still needed a home for coaching, capability development and consistency across squads.
Quarterly business reviewsTeams made achievements, learning, next goals and cross-team dependencies more transparent rather than relying only on traditional reporting chains.
End-to-end thinkingCommercial, product, UX, data and engineering perspectives were brought closer to the same customer problem and definition of success.
Different patterns for different workIndependent functions such as legal, finance and operational risk were not simply forced into the same squad structure.
04 · WHAT HAPPENED NEXT

Useful improvements—and new tensions

A credible case should not stop at “the transformation worked.”

ING leaders reported improvements in time to market, employee engagement and productivity after the new model was introduced. McKinsey's 2017 interview described roughly 350 nine-person squads across 13 tribes at that stage of the Netherlands transformation.

But the model also created questions that are more interesting than the headline. How much autonomy can teams have without fragmenting strategy? How should goals stretch teams without becoming top-down targets? How do career development and craft excellence work when traditional managerial ladders are weakened? Which functions should remain independent?

Those questions are not evidence of failure. They are evidence that organizational design moves constraints rather than eliminating them. Faster local decisions create a stronger need for transparent alignment, capability development and clear interfaces.

05 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

What is actually transferable?

Copy the principle only after understanding the system condition it addressed.

Flow over handoffsWhen value crosses many organizational boundaries, queues and coordination cost become part of lead time.
Cross-functionalityA team can act faster when enough capability to achieve its mission is close to the work rather than waiting in a chain of specialists.
Autonomy requires alignmentLocal decision authority works only when purpose, outcome measures, constraints and interfaces are sufficiently clear.
Governance must evolve tooChanging team ceremonies while preserving every old approval, funding and reporting mechanism limits the system-level benefit.
Context beats templatesIndependent risk and control functions may need different structures from customer-facing product development.
Inspect the operating modelIf the same dependencies, decisions and handoffs remain, renaming teams will not create meaningful agility.
06 · TRANSFER, DON'T COPY

Translate ING's pattern into your environment

The question is not “should we have tribes?” It is “which constraint are we trying to remove?”

07 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions for an Agile Coach or transformation leader

Use the case as a mirror for your own system rather than as a blueprint.

ObserveWhere does an important customer outcome cross the most teams, functions or approval boundaries today?
ChallengeWhich handoff exists because of a real control need—and which exists only because “that is how the organization is structured”?
ExploreIf a team received more decision authority tomorrow, what context, skills or guardrails would it immediately need?
ExperimentWhat is the smallest end-to-end customer problem you could give to one multidisciplinary team without reorganizing the entire company?
ReflectAre you trying to copy the appearance of an Agile organization—or remove a specific system constraint?
