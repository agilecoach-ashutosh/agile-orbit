PIVOT & EXPERIMENT · PRODUCT

Pivot, persevere—or stop? Follow the evidence.

A pivot is not a fashionable synonym for changing your mind. The useful pattern is narrower: preserve the learning, question the current solution, and redirect investment toward stronger evidence of value. Slack, Instagram and Netflix illustrate three different forms of adaptation.

01 · THREE DIFFERENT PIVOTS

Adaptation can happen at different levels

Not every pivot is the same. Sometimes the useful product is hidden inside the original one; sometimes the delivery model changes; sometimes the underlying customer job stays constant while the technology changes.

Slack — product extracted from a failed gameSlack began as an internal communication tool built while the Tiny Speck team was developing the game Glitch. When the game shut down, the collaboration tool became the product.
Instagram — radical narrowingThe early Burbn product contained multiple capabilities. The team simplified around photo sharing, comments and likes, removing much of the clutter rather than expanding it.
Netflix — business model transitionNetflix began with DVDs by mail, introduced streaming in 2007 and progressively moved the customer experience from physical distribution toward digital delivery.
The common patternThe winning move was not “change everything.” Each case retained something valuable: a collaboration capability, a behaviour users loved, or a core entertainment job.
02 · DECISION POINT

Your original product is struggling, but one behaviour is surprisingly strong. What do you do?

Imagine a product with declining overall usage. Yet analytics and interviews show that customers repeatedly use one small capability, recommend it to others, and improvise workflows around it. The team has already invested heavily in the original concept.

There is no perfect option. Choose the path you would defend.

Option B is usually the most evidence-driven move.

The lesson from successful pivots is not “pivot fast.” It is learn fast enough to know what deserves a larger commitment. Strong teams isolate the signal, test whether it represents a durable customer problem or behaviour, and then redirect investment deliberately.

03 · WHAT TO LOOK FOR

Evidence that a pivot may be warranted

A pivot should be explainable through changed evidence, not executive mood.

Repeated user behaviourUsers consistently choose one capability even when the broader proposition underperforms.
Pull, not persuasionCustomers return, recommend, workaround limitations or ask for more without extraordinary selling effort.
A clearer customer jobThe strongest use case can be explained as a specific problem worth solving rather than a miscellaneous feature.
Cheap falsificationThe team can test the new hypothesis through a smaller experiment before reorganizing the whole company around it.
Common misconceptionA pivot means abandoning the strategy whenever growth is difficult.
→
More useful lessonA pivot is a structured change in direction based on learning while preserving useful knowledge, capability or customer insight.
04 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

What transfers beyond startups?

Large enterprises also need the ability to change direction, but they usually pay a higher coordination cost when evidence contradicts committed plans.

PatternCopy this principleAvoid copying blindly
SlackLook for valuable internal capabilities created while solving your own problem.Do not assume every internal tool is a marketable product.
InstagramRemove features when simplification sharpens the user value proposition.Do not equate minimalism with product-market fit.
NetflixSeparate the enduring customer job from the technology used to deliver it.Do not abandon a profitable channel before a replacement can carry the business.
Enterprise portfolioFund learning in stages and make stop/pivot decisions explicit.Do not reward teams only for completing the originally approved scope.
05 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions for product teams and leaders

ObserveWhich user behaviour is stronger than the story we tell ourselves about the product?
EvidenceWhat would need to be true for this signal to justify redirecting investment?
BiasWhich sunk costs are influencing our willingness to stop or simplify?
ExperimentWhat is the cheapest test that distinguishes a genuine opportunity from an interesting anomaly?
GovernanceDoes our funding model make it easier to continue a weak plan than to admit the evidence changed?
