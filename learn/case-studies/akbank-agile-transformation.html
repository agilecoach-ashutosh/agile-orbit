Akbank: scaling Scrum means scaling the learning system

Akbank's IT transformation grew from a waterfall environment into more than 100 Scrum Teams. The interesting problem is not the team count. It is what happens when planning, coaching, Product Ownership, Scrum Master capability and coordination all have to evolve at the same time.

01 · THE SITUATION

A few Agile teams are one problem. An Agile organisation is another.

Scaling creates second-order problems that are invisible in a pilot.

According to Scrum.org's Akbank case study, the bank began its Agile journey in 2010 and eventually grew to around 950 people and 143 Scrum Teams. That scale changes the nature of the transformation. You can no longer rely on a handful of coaches to personally solve every problem or on one annual master plan to remain useful across changing priorities.

The case highlights several responses: replacing a yearly master plan with a monthly focus called “The Spotlight,” developing internal Agile coaches, strengthening Scrum Master and Product Owner capability, and building communities and internal events to spread learning.

Plan half-lifeAnnual plans become stale while teams are asked to inspect and adapt every few weeks.
Coach bottleneckIf every difficult situation requires an external or central coach, the transformation cannot scale.
Role dilutionRapid growth can turn Product Owners and Scrum Masters into titles without enough capability behind them.
Local optimizationMore teams can create more dependencies and coordination overhead if the system around them does not change.
02 · DECISION POINT

Your pilot worked. Leadership wants 100 teams. What do you scale first?

You have limited experienced coaches and a planning system designed for annual commitments. What is the highest-leverage move?

Choose what would reduce dependence on the transformation team over time.

Akbank's reported pattern is closest to Option C.

The case describes a move away from a yearly master plan toward a monthly focus, plus deliberate investment in internal coaches, Scrum Master and Product Owner capability, communities and internal Agile events. In other words, scale was treated partly as a capability-distribution problem, not just a team-rollout problem.

03 · WHAT CHANGED

The transformation infrastructure became part of the product

At scale, the support system around teams becomes a design problem in its own right.

Monthly planning focusA shorter prioritisation rhythm reduced the mismatch between annual commitments and changing delivery reality.
Internal Agile coachesCapability was grown inside the organisation so improvement did not rely indefinitely on external experts.
Scrum Master communityPractitioners could share patterns, problems and learning rather than solving everything team by team.
Product Owner developmentScaling teams required scaling product decision capability, not merely creating more backlog administrators.
Internal eventsLearning was socialised across the organisation, increasing the number of people able to teach and challenge practices.
Coaching toolsThe case notes that increasing team numbers pushed Akbank to create reusable coaching mechanisms rather than bespoke interventions only.
COMMON MISREAD“Scaling Agile means increasing the number of Scrum Teams.”
VS
MORE USEFUL LESSONTeam count creates new constraints. Planning, coaching, role capability and learning networks must scale too—or the organisation simply multiplies local Scrum.
04 · AGILE ORBIT LENS

What to transfer without copying Akbank

Shorten the planning feedback loopIf teams inspect every Sprint but investment decisions change once a year, agility is capped above the team level.
Build capability where work happensThe goal of coaching is not permanent coach dependency. Mature systems create more people who can diagnose and improve their own work.
Communities are scaling mechanismsThey spread tacit knowledge horizontally without routing every decision through hierarchy.
Roles need depth, not labelsOne hundred Product Owners with weak product authority do not create one hundred empowered product teams.
05 · COACH THE SITUATION

Questions for a large transformation

ObserveWhich decisions still happen annually even though teams are expected to adapt every Sprint?
ConstraintWhere has the coaching function itself become a queue?
CapabilityWhat can your Scrum Masters and Product Owners solve independently today that required an expert six months ago?
ScaleWhich recurring team-level problem should become a reusable organisational learning asset?
