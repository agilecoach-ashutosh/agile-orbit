Scrum Event Facilitation

Use the event's purpose to shape the conversation. The facilitator protects participation, focus and transparency; the Scrum Team owns the decisions and outcomes.

Important: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective are Scrum Events. Product Backlog Refinement is an ongoing activity, so it is shown separately below as a supporting Scrum activity.
SCRUM EVENT

Sprint Planning

Purpose: initiate the Sprint by laying out why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done and how the chosen work will get done.

Facilitator focus

Keep the conversation anchored on a clear Sprint Goal, realistic capacity and shared understanding rather than turning planning into task assignment.

Before the event

  • Ensure the Product Backlog is transparent enough to discuss.
  • Bring relevant product context, evidence and known constraints.
  • Make capacity, dependencies and significant risks visible.

Suggested flow

  • Why is this Sprint valuable?
  • What Product Backlog Items support that outcome?
  • How will the Developers organize the work?
  • Confirm the Sprint Goal and major assumptions.

Powerful questions

  • What outcome would make this Sprint worth doing?
  • What are we assuming that could make this plan unrealistic?
  • What should we remove rather than overcommit?

Watch out for

  • Manager assigning tasks.
  • Planning every hour of the Sprint.
  • Treating the forecast as a fixed contract.
  • No meaningful Sprint Goal.

Success looks like

The team leaves with a coherent Sprint Goal, a credible forecast, a shared plan and enough clarity to start learning through execution.

SCRUM EVENT

Daily Scrum

Purpose: inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary.

Facilitator focus

Help the Developers inspect the plan together. Avoid turning it into a status report to the Scrum Master or manager.

Before the event

  • Make Sprint Goal and current work visible.
  • Keep the event short and focused.
  • Encourage Developers to own the format.

Suggested flow

  • Restate or visibly anchor on the Sprint Goal.
  • Inspect progress and emerging blockers.
  • Adapt the plan for the next day.
  • Take deep problem-solving outside the 15-minute event.

Powerful questions

  • Are we still on track toward the Sprint Goal?
  • What changed since yesterday that affects our plan?
  • Where should we collaborate or swarm today?

Watch out for

  • Round-robin reporting to one person.
  • Problem-solving every issue in the meeting.
  • Ignoring the Sprint Goal.
  • Facilitator becoming permanent chair.

Success looks like

The Developers leave with an adapted plan and clearer coordination for the next working day.

SCRUM EVENT

Sprint Review

Purpose: inspect the outcome of the Sprint with stakeholders and determine useful future adaptations.

Facilitator focus

Create a working conversation about product outcomes, evidence and what to do next—not a presentation theatre or sign-off meeting.

Before the event

  • Invite stakeholders who can contribute useful context.
  • Prepare usable product evidence, not slide-heavy status.
  • Make the Product Goal and recent learning visible.

Suggested flow

  • Reconnect to the Product Goal and Sprint outcome.
  • Inspect the Increment and relevant evidence.
  • Discuss what changed in users, market or constraints.
  • Adapt the Product Backlog and next options.

Powerful questions

  • What did we learn that changes our assumptions?
  • What changed for users or stakeholders?
  • Based on today's evidence, what should we do differently next?

Watch out for

  • Demo-only agenda.
  • Stakeholder approval gate.
  • Reporting output instead of inspecting outcomes.
  • No adaptation after feedback.

Success looks like

People leave with better shared understanding of the product and clear adaptations to future work or priorities.

SCRUM EVENT

Sprint Retrospective

Purpose: plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness by inspecting how the last Sprint went.

Facilitator focus

Create enough safety and structure for useful truth to surface, then help the team convert insight into a small number of meaningful improvements.

Before the event

  • Choose a format that fits the team's current situation.
  • Bring relevant evidence where helpful.
  • Protect enough time for deciding actions, not only collecting comments.

Suggested flow

  • Set the stage and establish safety.
  • Gather data and perspectives.
  • Generate insights and patterns.
  • Decide one or two improvements.
  • Close with ownership and follow-up.

Powerful questions

  • What pattern matters more than the loudest complaint?
  • What is within our control to change?
  • What evidence will tell us the improvement worked?

Watch out for

  • Blame or personal criticism.
  • Repeating the same format indefinitely.
  • Long complaint lists with no action.
  • Actions with no owner or follow-up.

Success looks like

The team reaches a clearer shared understanding and commits to a small, testable improvement it can inspect later.

SUPPORTING SCRUM ACTIVITY

Product Backlog Refinement

Purpose: break down and further define Product Backlog Items so the backlog remains transparent and ready for future decisions.

Facilitator focus

Enable shared understanding without turning refinement into a mandatory recurring meeting or a solution-design handoff.

Before the activity

  • Choose items that need useful conversation now.
  • Bring product context and relevant examples.
  • Invite the people whose knowledge is needed.

Suggested flow

  • Clarify the problem or outcome.
  • Explore questions, assumptions and dependencies.
  • Split or reshape items when useful.
  • Capture enough understanding for future selection and planning.

Powerful questions

  • What user or business problem are we solving?
  • What is still ambiguous?
  • How could we make this smaller and still valuable?

Watch out for

  • Treating refinement as a formal Scrum Event.
  • Refining months of work upfront.
  • Only the Product Owner talking.
  • Estimating without understanding.

Success looks like

The Product Backlog becomes clearer and more transparent without creating excessive inventory or pretending future work is fully known.
