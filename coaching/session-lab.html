PRACTICE LENS

What are you trying to practise?

A strong response is contextual. Use these four lenses to notice what your intervention is doing—not to hunt for one universally correct question.

01 · AgreementStay with the client's purpose

Clarify what would make the conversation useful before deciding what the client “really needs.”

02 · ListeningUse their language

Follow words, emotion, energy, values, assumptions and shifts rather than replacing them with your preferred frame.

03 · AwarenessOpen thinking

Explore meaning, patterns and perspective without turning your interpretation into the answer.

04 · GrowthReturn ownership

Help the client integrate learning and design the next move without making action the coach's project.

How to practise

Pause before selecting.

1. Name your impulseDo you want to solve, reassure, diagnose, challenge, teach, rescue or genuinely explore?
2. Find the client's wordsWhich word, emotion, assumption or shift is actually present in what they just said?
3. Check the agreementWould your response still serve what the client said they wanted from this conversation?
4. Notice the size of your interventionCould a shorter observation, one clean question or a little silence create more space?
5. Let the client surprise youIf your question only works when the client gives the answer you expect, it may be leading.
Feedback in this lab is reflective, not an ICF credentialing assessment. Tone, timing, relationship and client context can change what is useful.
DEBRIEF

Turn a simulated conversation into deliberate practice.

After one scenario, do not ask “Did I get the right answers?” Ask what your choices reveal about your coaching habits.

Where did I lead?

Which option felt attractive because I already had a solution, interpretation or preferred outcome?

What did I miss?

What client language, emotion, contradiction, value or change in energy could have been explored more lightly?

What will I repeat?

Choose one observable behaviour for your next session: one question at a time, more silence, cleaner agreement or lighter observations.

MICRO-DRILLS

Practise one skill at a time.

The 10-second pause

After the client finishes, wait ten seconds before speaking. Notice what appears in you and what appears in them.

One-question rule

For five minutes, ask only one question at a time. Remove every “and” that creates a second question.

Client-language drill

Build your next question around one exact word the client used instead of introducing a new label.

Observation, not diagnosis

Practise saying what you noticed—pace, repetition, shift or contradiction—without explaining why it happened.

Re-contract in motion

When a deeper topic appears, ask whether exploring it would serve the outcome the client wants today.

End without rescuing

Let the client name what they are taking away and what, if anything, they want to do next.

Advice detector

Rewrite “Have you tried…?” into a question that expands options without smuggling your recommendation inside it.

Three interpretations

When you think you know what something means, generate three other plausible meanings before speaking.
