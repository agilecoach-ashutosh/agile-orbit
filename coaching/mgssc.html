Marshall Goldsmith Stakeholder Centered Coaching

Behaviour change becomes more visible when the people who experience the behaviour are part of the learning loop. MGSSC brings stakeholder input, reflection, feedforward, action and follow-up into the development process.

AT A GLANCE

Change is not observed only inside the coaching room

The model places stakeholders around the leader into the development process. Their perspective helps surface how behaviour is experienced, while repeated follow-up keeps attention on observable change rather than insight alone.

THE CYCLE

Seven moves that keep change connected to stakeholders

The sequence below follows the MGSSC cycle presented in the supplied coaching learning material. Treat it as a repeating loop rather than a one-time checklist.

1

Ask

Begin by inviting stakeholder input rather than assuming you already know how the behaviour is being experienced.

“What would help me become more effective here?”
2

Listen

Hear the stakeholder perspective without immediately explaining, defending or correcting what they experienced.

Listen for impact, not only intent.
3

Thank

Acknowledge the effort and candour involved in giving input. Appreciation keeps the channel for future feedback open.

Receive the input before deciding what to do with it.
4

Think

Pause and reflect on what was heard. Look for recurring themes, tensions and behaviour that may be worth changing.

What deserves attention rather than reaction?
5

Respond

Use feedforward and respond constructively, keeping the conversation oriented toward what could be done differently going forward.

Move from defending the past to shaping the future.
6

Change

Turn learning into observable behaviour. The development effort becomes meaningful when stakeholders can experience something different.

What behaviour will be visible, not merely intended?
7

Follow Up

Return to stakeholders, ask what they are noticing and continue the cycle. Follow-up makes development an ongoing practice.

“What are you noticing now, and what could I improve next?”
WHAT MAKES IT DISTINCTIVE

Three ideas at the centre of the model

👥

Involve Stakeholders

The leader's stakeholders become active participants in the development process rather than leaving all observation and reflection inside the coach–client relationship.

➡️

Emphasise Feedforward

The learning material highlights feedforward: future-oriented suggestions that help focus attention on what the leader can do differently next.

🔄

Change Behaviour & Perceptions

Behaviour change matters, and so does whether stakeholders actually experience that change over time. Follow-up helps make both visible.

COACHING LENS

The coach is not the only judge of progress

MGSSC deliberately widens the feedback loop. Insight in a session is useful, but the stronger test is whether different behaviour is experienced by the people around the leader. That is why asking, listening and following up remain part of the cycle.
