PRACTICAL COACHING INTERVENTION

Dependency & Constraint Coaching

Surface what is slowing the team down. Make hidden waiting, hand-offs, approvals and organisational constraints visible, understand their impact on flow and pressure, then decide what to control, influence, escalate or consciously accept.

Start the intervention ↓Powerful questions ↓
Dependencies are not automatically bad.

Some are necessary. The coaching goal is to identify which dependencies repeatedly delay value, create pressure or reduce autonomy—and then choose an intentional response rather than relying on heroics.

SURFACE THE SYSTEM

What kind of dependency is this?

Give the team language for patterns that otherwise get described only as “blocked”, “waiting” or “urgent”.

🧠

Competency / Skill

The team cannot complete the work because required knowledge or capability sits elsewhere.

Example: only one specialist team understands the legacy settlement engine.
🏗️

Architecture / System

A change in one component requires coordinated changes in another component or service.

Example: a portfolio UI release depends on a shared pricing API change.
🔄

Process / Workflow

Work cannot finish until another process, stage or hand-off has completed.

Example: compliance review starts only after development is complete.
✅

Decision / Approval

Progress stops while the team waits for a decision, sign-off or governance forum.

Example: architecture approval is required before implementation can proceed.
🔐

Rights / Authorization

The team lacks the permission, access or authority needed to complete its own work.

Example: only a central operations team can deploy to production.
🤝

External Team

Another internal team must deliver something before this team can complete its outcome.

Example: the data platform team must expose a new customer-risk attribute.
🌐

Vendor / Third Party

Progress depends on a supplier, partner, market utility or external organisation.

Example: release waits for market-data vendor certification.
🧪

Environment / Tooling / Data

Shared environments, test data, tooling or infrastructure availability constrain progress.

Example: SIT is available only in limited windows and is shared by multiple teams.
INVESTIGATE

Dependency Investigation Canvas

Move the conversation from “we have too many dependencies” to evidence about where flow is actually being constrained.

QuestionWhat to capture
What are we waiting for?The dependency, approval, capability, information, environment or decision.
Who or what do we depend on?Team, role, system, process, vendor, governance forum or individual.
Why does this dependency exist?Architecture, policy, capability gap, ownership boundary, risk control, history or habit.
How often does it affect us?Rare, occasional, frequent or effectively every Sprint / delivery cycle.
How long do we typically wait?Hours, days or weeks—use actual examples where possible.
What gets delayed?Story, feature, release, customer outcome, regulatory commitment or learning.
What happens while we wait?More WIP, context switching, rework, escalation, idle work, hidden queues or new work started.
Can the team remove it?Yes, partially, no—or unknown until explored.
What if nothing changes?Delivery, customer, risk, quality, morale or business impact.
What evidence do we have?Blocked days, aging work, cycle time, missed dates, queue length, rework, incident history.
CHOOSE THE RESPONSE

Control · Influence · Escalate · Accept / Manage

The useful coaching outcome is not merely naming the dependency. It is deciding what response fits the system.

CONTROL

We can change this ourselves

Use when the team has enough authority or capability to reduce the constraint directly.

  • What are we waiting for that we could learn to do ourselves?
  • Which permission, skill or process could move inside the team?
  • What recurring dependency exists because “we have always done it this way”?
  • What is the smallest experiment we can run next Sprint?
INFLUENCE

We need another team, but can improve the relationship

Use when the dependency is real but the way teams coordinate can be improved.

  • Is the dependency unavoidable, or only the current way of managing it?
  • Can we create clearer interfaces, expectations or service boundaries?
  • Can the teams refine or plan together earlier?
  • Can dependency work be pulled forward before it becomes critical?
ESCALATE

The system needs management attention

Use when the problem is bigger than the team's authority and requires policy, structure or leadership change.

  • How many teams experience the same constraint?
  • What organisational policy or decision right is creating the delay?
  • Is governance protecting against a real risk, or mostly creating waiting?
  • What evidence would help leadership understand the system cost?
ACCEPT / MANAGE

This constraint is legitimate—for now

Some dependencies protect real regulatory, operational or business risks and cannot simply be removed.

  • What risk does this dependency protect us from?
  • Can we make the waiting predictable?
  • Can we reduce its frequency or impact?
  • What early warning would stop it surprising the team?
Important distinction: the goal is not to remove every dependency. It is to stop being repeatedly surprised by dependencies the system already knows about.
COACHING QUESTIONS

Questions that make the invisible visible

Use inquiry before jumping to solutions. The questions below help the team see waiting, coupling and pressure as system signals.

Surface the hidden system

  • Where does work spend more time waiting than being worked on?
  • What does the team regularly say “we are waiting for”?
  • Who do we repeatedly need before we can finish?
  • Which work looks “in progress” but is actually blocked?
  • What dependency has become so normal that nobody questions it anymore?
  • Where does work leave the team and later come back?

Understand the impact

  • What happens to other work while this item waits?
  • Does waiting cause us to start more work?
  • How much WIP exists because something else is blocked?
  • Which dependency creates the most context switching?
  • Which delays affect customers rather than only internal milestones?
  • What percentage of our cycle time is actually waiting?

Challenge the system

  • Why does this dependency exist?
  • What would need to be true for us not to need it?
  • Is this a real dependency or an organisational habit?
  • Are we dependent on a capability, a person, a permission or a decision?
  • Could capability move instead of work moving?
  • What would happen if the team owned this capability themselves?

Move toward action

  • Which dependency could we reduce without waiting for a reorganisation?
  • What experiment would give us evidence within two Sprints?
  • Who needs to be part of the conversation that is currently missing?
  • What can we make explicit before work starts?
  • What should we stop starting when this dependency blocks us?
  • How will we know the situation has improved?

Expose the pressure behind the chaos

  • Why does the team always feel under pressure?
  • Is the team actually overloaded—or is work accumulating because it cannot flow?
  • How many things are currently waiting on somebody else?
  • What work did we start because another item became blocked?
  • How much urgent work is caused by previously unresolved dependencies?
  • Where are people compensating manually for a broken system?
  • What recurring organisational problem is currently being absorbed by individual heroics?

FACILITATE IT

60–90 minute coaching session

A lightweight structure for turning frustration into evidence, choices and experiments.

5 MIN · FRAME

Set the purpose

We are not here to blame another team. We are here to understand what constrains our ability to deliver value.

15 MIN · SURFACE

Generate dependencies

Silent brainstorm first, then group dependencies by type. Include decisions, permissions and waiting—not only technical dependencies.

15 MIN · EVIDENCE

Explore impact

Add frequency, wait time, affected work and observable consequences such as WIP, context switching or missed outcomes.

15 MIN · CLASSIFY

Choose the response

Place each significant dependency under Control, Influence, Escalate or Accept / Manage.

10 MIN · PRIORITISE

Find the few that matter

Use exposure scoring or simple dot voting. Do not leave with a list of twenty equal problems.

10–20 MIN · ACT

Design experiments

Agree a small action, owner, evidence to watch and review date for the top one to three dependencies.

Take the canvas into your next session

Copy a lightweight facilitation template with the questions, classification and action fields.

“A team cannot improve a constraint it cannot see. Management cannot improve a system problem that remains hidden inside team heroics.”

The coaching outcome is transparency: make the cost of waiting visible, separate local problems from systemic ones, and create an intentional response instead of normalising constant pressure.
