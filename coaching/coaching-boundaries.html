Coach, advise or refer?

Strong coaching is not about refusing every form of expertise. It is about knowing which role you are in, making role changes explicit and not disguising advice as coaching.

Stay coaching

Coach

The client owns the decision, the issue benefits from reflection and there is no single correct answer you need to provide.

Change hats

Mentor / Teach / Advise

The client explicitly wants expertise, instruction or experience. Name the role change, get permission, then return ownership to the client.

Different support

Refer

The need falls outside your competence or requires a qualified clinical, legal, financial, safeguarding or other specialist professional.

Client: “Tell me exactly how I should structure tomorrow's presentation.”

This may be a moment for teaching or mentoring if you have relevant expertise and the client wants it. You can say: “I can switch hats and offer a suggestion. Would that be useful?”

Client: “I know what I should do, but I keep avoiding it.”

This may still be a rich coaching topic. The value may be in exploring what the avoidance protects, what belief is operating or what choice the client has not acknowledged.

Client presents a need beyond coaching scope.

Do not stretch coaching to prove usefulness. Name the boundary respectfully and help the client consider more appropriate professional support.

Coach self-check: Am I staying in coaching because it serves the client—or because changing hats or referring feels uncomfortable for me?
