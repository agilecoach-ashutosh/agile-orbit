Team Coaching Fundamentals

The coaching client is not just a collection of individuals. It is the team-in-context: its purpose, relationships, stakeholder expectations, patterns and capacity to learn together.

Shift the unit of attention

From person → pattern

Instead of “Who is the problem?”, ask what interaction keeps recreating the problem.

From team → system

Who commissions the team? Who receives value from it? What dependencies shape its choices?

From harmony → effectiveness

A healthy team does not avoid tension; it can use difference and conflict in service of purpose.

From event → learning cycle

Team coaching is not a one-off workshop. The team observes itself, experiments and learns over time.

Questions for the team coach

  • What is this team here to achieve that individuals cannot achieve separately?
  • Who are the team's critical stakeholders, and what do they need from the team?
  • What recurring interaction pattern helps or hinders the team?
  • What is the team not discussing because the social cost feels too high?
  • How will the team notice whether coaching is changing collective performance?
