Ladder of Inference

Help a client notice how quickly the mind can move from observable data to selected meaning, assumptions, conclusions, beliefs and action.

1. Observable dataWhat could a camera or transcript record?
2. Selected dataWhat did I notice, emphasise or ignore?
3. MeaningWhat meaning did I add?
4. AssumptionsWhat did I assume was true?
5. ConclusionsWhat did I decide this means?
6. BeliefsWhat belief about me, them or the system gets reinforced?
7. ActionWhat do I now do because I believe that conclusion?

Example

Observable: My manager cancelled our 1:1 and attended another meeting.

Selected: I focus on the fact that the other meeting went ahead.

Meaning: My work is less important.

Assumption: My manager does not value me.

Conclusion: My career here is going nowhere.

Action: I stop volunteering ideas.

Come back down the ladder

  • What did a camera actually record?
  • What information did you select?
  • What information might be missing?
  • What else could the same facts mean?
  • Which assumption are you most certain about?
  • What would you do differently if that assumption were only one possible explanation?

The Ladder of Inference is commonly attributed to Chris Argyris and was popularised in organisational learning work. Agile Orbit uses it here as a reflection structure for coaching rather than as a diagnostic instrument.
