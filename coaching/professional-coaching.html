CLIENT-LED COACHING

Professional Coaching

Create a thinking partnership that helps the client increase awareness, make choices and design their own way forward. The coach brings presence, curiosity and process — not the answer.

CONVERSATION ARC

A simple coaching flow

Use structure lightly. Follow the client rather than forcing the conversation through a script.

01

Contract

What would make this conversation useful? Clarify the topic, desired outcome and what the client wants from the coach.

02

Explore

Listen for facts, emotions, assumptions, values, language patterns, tensions and what is not yet being said.

03

Evoke awareness

Use observations, silence and questions that help the client see a new perspective rather than directing them to yours.

04

Design forward

Invite the client to choose what they want to do, learn, stop, test or pay attention to next.

05

Close

Ask what changed in their thinking, what they are taking away and how they want to hold themselves accountable.

THREAD

Stay with the client

Keep returning to the client's words, energy, outcome and meaning. Do not replace their agenda with the coach's curiosity.

CORE DISCIPLINES

What strong coaching sounds like

Listen beyond content

Notice changes in pace, tone, emotion, certainty, contradiction, metaphor and energy. Reflect what you notice without diagnosing.

Ask, then stay quiet

Prefer one clean question over a stack of questions. Give the client enough silence to think instead of rescuing the pause.

Offer observations lightly

Use language such as “I notice…” or “I am hearing…” and let the client decide whether the observation is useful.

Protect client agency

The client's insight, choice and action matter more than the coach sounding clever. Do not manufacture commitment for them.

Useful self-check: am I helping the client think more deeply, or am I trying to prove that I can solve the problem?
QUESTION DECK

One question. Then listen.

COACHING QUESTION

What would make this conversation genuinely useful for you?

QUESTION BANK

Prompts by coaching intent

Clarify outcome

What would you like to be different by the end of this conversation?

How will you know this has been useful?

Deepen awareness

What are you noticing as you say that?

What feels most important underneath the story?

Challenge assumptions

What are you treating as true that may only be an assumption?

What else could this mean?

Explore choice

What options become available if you stop trying to control that part?

What choice are you making by doing nothing?

Move to action

What do you want to do with this awareness?

What is the smallest meaningful next step?

Close learning

What are you taking away about yourself?

What do you want to remember when this situation appears again?

BOUNDARIES

Coach — or consciously change hats

Professional coaching is more powerful when the boundary between coaching and advice is explicit.

STAY IN COACHING

Coach when…

The client owns the decision.

The value is in reflection, awareness or choice.

There is no single correct answer.

The client has enough capability to generate options.

CHANGE HATS

Be explicit when…

The client asks for expertise, teaching or mentoring.

A policy, safety, legal or ethical requirement requires direct information.

You have decision authority that cannot honestly be disguised as coaching.

Another professional service is more appropriate than coaching.
