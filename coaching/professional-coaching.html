What coaching is — and what it is not

If you are new to coaching, begin here. You do not need a perfectly formed goal before speaking with a coach.

Useful for both🧭

What is coaching?

A thinking partnership. The coach helps the client explore what matters, notice patterns and assumptions, create awareness and make their own choices. The coach owns the process; the client owns the life, decision and action.

Coachee💭

What can I bring?

A decision, goal, difficult relationship, transition, recurring pattern, leadership challenge, confidence issue, competing priorities — or simply: “Something feels stuck and I don't know why.”

Coachee🔄

What happens in a session?

You agree what would make the conversation useful, explore the situation and what sits beneath it, notice new possibilities, then decide what — if anything — you want to do with the awareness.

Coach🤝

What does the coach actually do?

Creates safety, listens beyond the story, stays curious, notices patterns, offers observations without attachment, asks clean questions, allows silence and protects the client's agency.

CoachingHelps you think, discover and choose your own way forward.
MentoringDraws on another person's experience to offer guidance and perspective.
ConsultingUses expertise to diagnose a problem and recommend solutions.
TherapyAddresses mental-health, clinical or therapeutic needs with an appropriately qualified professional.

COACHING MODELS

Frameworks that support — not control — the conversation

Open a model to see when it may help and sample questions. Models are scaffolding; follow the client, not the acronym.

Coach
GROW
Goal → Reality → Options → Will / Way Forward
Useful for goal-focused conversations and forward movement.
Try:
  • Goal: What would you like to achieve?
  • Reality: What is happening now?
  • Options: What possibilities are available?
  • Will: What do you want to do next?
Coach
TGROW
Topic → Goal → Reality → Options → Will
Useful when the topic is broad, fuzzy or still emerging.
Try:
  • Topic: What feels worth exploring today?
  • Goal: What would make this useful?
  • Reality: What is true now?
  • Options: What else could you do or see?
  • Will: What will you take forward?
Coach
CLEAR
Contract → Listen → Explore → Action → Review
Useful for developmental conversations with room for deeper exploration.
Try:
  • Contract: What do we need to agree for this conversation?
  • Listen: Stay with the client's language and meaning.
  • Explore: What is underneath the obvious issue?
  • Action: What does the client choose?
  • Review: What changed or was learned?
Coach
OSKAR
Outcome → Scaling → Know-how → Affirm & Action → Review
Useful for solution-focused conversations and visible progress.
Try:
  • Outcome: What would better look like?
  • Scaling: Where are you now from 1–10?
  • Know-how: What is already helping?
  • Action: What small move would shift the number?
  • Review: What has improved since last time?
Coach
Solution-Focused
Preferred future → Exceptions → Resources → Small steps
Useful when the client is trapped in problem analysis.
Try:
  • If this were better, what would you notice first?
  • When is the problem less powerful?
  • What are you already doing that helps?
  • What is one small experiment worth trying?
Coach
Appreciative Inquiry
Discover → Dream → Design → Destiny / Deliver
Useful for strengths-based change and possibility thinking.
Try:
  • Discover: When has this worked at its best?
  • Dream: What would an ideal future look like?
  • Design: What conditions would make that possible?
  • Destiny: What will sustain movement toward it?
Watch-out: If you are thinking harder about the next step in your model than listening to the client, the model has become the agenda.

ICF COACHING COMPASS

Four domains to orient the coach

A practical summary of the four domains in the 2025 ICF Core Competencies. Use this as a compass for reflective practice, not a session checklist.

DOMAIN 01

Foundation

Work ethically, stay client-centred, keep learning and notice how your own assumptions, emotions and context influence the coaching.

DOMAIN 02

Co-Creating the Relationship

Build trust, clarify agreements, create safety and partner with the client about how the coaching relationship works.

DOMAIN 03

Communicating Effectively

Stay present, listen to words and what surrounds them, reflect observations and ask questions that invite awareness.

DOMAIN 04

Cultivating Learning & Growth

Help the client integrate insight, explore options and choose learning or action they genuinely own.

Reference: International Coaching Federation — 2025 Core Competencies ↗

ETHICS + BOUNDARIES

Professional coaching needs clear edges

Trust grows when the client understands the relationship, confidentiality and when the coach is changing roles.

Useful for both

Confidentiality

Clarify what is confidential, what may be shared, with whom, and any legal or safety-related limits before assumptions are made.

Useful for both

Coaching agreement

Agree purpose, roles, expectations, logistics and how the relationship will work — including sponsor arrangements when relevant.

Coach

Conflicts of interest

Notice personal, professional, financial or organisational interests that could affect the coaching relationship and address them transparently.

Coach

Know when to refer

Do not stretch coaching beyond your competence. Another qualified professional may be more appropriate for clinical, legal, financial or other specialist needs.

Useful for both

AI, recordings & notes

Be explicit about technology, recordings, storage, access and how information is used. Consent should be informed rather than assumed.

Useful for both

Ending the relationship

A coaching relationship is not an obligation to continue forever. Review value, progress and whether coaching remains useful.

The current ICF Code of Ethics took effect on 1 April 2025. Reference: ICF Code of Ethics ↗

COACH OR ADVISE?

Notice when you are solving instead of coaching

Advice is not automatically wrong. The issue is pretending advice is coaching. Change hats consciously and with permission when expertise is actually needed.

Coach

Client: “I'm thinking about resigning. Do you think I should?”

Advice“You should wait until you have another offer.”
Coaching“What matters most to you about this decision?”
Coach

Client: “My manager doesn't trust me.”

Advice“You should explain the situation and ask for more autonomy.”
Coaching“What makes you conclude that trust is the issue?”
Coach

Client: “I keep missing my own priorities.”

Advice“Block two hours every morning and turn notifications off.”
Coaching“What keeps winning when your priorities lose?”
