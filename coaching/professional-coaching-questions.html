PRACTICE MODE

What could you ask next?

There is no single “correct” question. Pick one and reveal what conversational direction it might create.

Before asking

What did the client actually say? What are you curious about because it serves their exploration—not because you want to demonstrate insight?

After asking

Stop. Listen. Let the client finish the thought. Do not stack another question simply because the pause feels uncomfortable.

If the question misses

Let it miss. The client can say “that's not it” and redirect. Partnership matters more than having the perfect question.

QUESTION TRAPS

When a good-looking question stops being coaching

Hidden advice“Have you thought about delegating more?” often contains the coach's preferred answer inside the question.
Stacked questions“What happened, how did you feel, and what will you do?” gives the client three directions at once.
Interrogation modeA rapid sequence of questions can make the session feel like an interview instead of a thinking partnership.
Jumping to actionIf awareness is still emerging, “What will you do?” may close exploration too early.
Ignoring client languageA generic powerful question can be weaker than simply asking what one important word means for this client.
Attachment to impactA coach cannot force a breakthrough. Ask cleanly, stay unattached, and work with whatever the client does with it.
