One question. Then listen.

A question is not powerful because it sounds clever. It becomes useful when it helps this client notice something, explore beyond current thinking, make meaning or choose what comes next.

ONE AT A TIMEAsk one clean question, then give the client enough space to think and respond.
USE THEIR LANGUAGEBuild from the client's words and meaning instead of replacing them with your interpretation.
FOLLOW ENERGYNotice emotion, hesitation, emphasis, shifts in pace and what suddenly becomes alive.
DON'T RESCUE THE SILENCEA pause can be thinking time. The next useful move may be no question at all.

Questions you can scan during practice

No questions match that search. Try a broader word or choose another intent.

Before asking

What did the client actually say? What are you curious about because it serves their exploration—not because you want to demonstrate insight?

After asking

Stop. Listen. Let the client finish the thought. Do not stack another question simply because the pause feels uncomfortable.

If the question misses

Let it miss. The client can say “that's not it” and redirect. Partnership matters more than having the perfect question.

QUESTION TRAPS

When a good-looking question stops being coaching

Hidden advice“Have you thought about delegating more?” often contains the coach's preferred answer inside the question.
Stacked questions“What happened, how did you feel, and what will you do?” gives the client three directions at once.
Interrogation modeA rapid sequence of questions can make the session feel like an interview instead of a thinking partnership.
Jumping to actionIf awareness is still emerging, “What will you do?” may close exploration too early.
Ignoring the client's wordsA generic powerful question can be weaker than simply asking, “What does ‘trapped’ mean for you?”
Attachment to impactA coach cannot force a breakthrough. Ask cleanly, stay unattached, and work with whatever the client does with it.
Professional reference: ICF's 2025 Core Competency 7, Evokes Awareness, includes questioning that explores the client's thinking, values, needs, wants and beliefs, helps the client move beyond current thinking, supports reframing and invites ideas for moving forward.
