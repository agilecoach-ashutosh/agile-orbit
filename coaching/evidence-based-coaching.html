Evidence-Based Coaching

Evidence should inform judgement—not become a new way for the coach to tell the client what to do.

What counts as useful evidence?

Research evidence

What do coaching and adjacent research suggest, and how strong or relevant is that evidence?

Professional expertise

What has the coach learned through training, supervision, deliberate practice and experience?

Client context

What matters to this client, in this culture, role, system and moment?

Observed outcomes

What is actually changing, and what feedback tells us whether the coaching is useful?

The Evidence Lens

1. ClaimWhat am I about to use or believe?
2. SourceWhere does that idea come from?
3. FitHow similar is the evidence context to this client?
4. LimitsWhat does the evidence not establish?
5. TestHow will we know whether it is useful here?

Common traps

  • “Research says…” as authority: using evidence to close conversation rather than deepen it.
  • Framework worship: treating a popular model as validated simply because many coaches use it.
  • Population mismatch: applying evidence from a different context as if it were directly transferable.
  • Outcome ambiguity: assuming the intervention worked without agreeing what meaningful change would look like.

Before using a model or tool

  • What evidence supports this idea?
  • What would make it relevant—or irrelevant—to this client?
  • Am I sharing information because the client asked, or because I feel more comfortable teaching than coaching?
  • What would I notice if this approach were not helping?
