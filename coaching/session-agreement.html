Agreement before exploration

The presenting story is not automatically the session outcome. Partner with the client on what would make this conversation useful, then stay willing to re-contract when something more important emerges.

01Arrive

What is present for the client now?

02Focus

What would be useful to work on today?

03Outcome

What would the client like to leave with?

04Explore

Work with what serves that purpose.

05Re-contract

If the direction shifts, check it together.

Questions that create a usable agreement

Clarify the purpose

  • What would make this conversation genuinely useful for you?
  • Of everything you have mentioned, what feels most important to work on today?
  • What would you like to be different by the end of this conversation?
  • What do you want to understand, decide or become clearer about?

Make value observable

  • How will you know this conversation has helped?
  • What would you leave with that you do not have right now?
  • If we used this time well, what might shift for you?
  • What would be enough progress for today?

Stay with the agreement

  • How does this connect with what you wanted from the session?
  • Is this still the most useful place for us to stay?
  • We seem to have reached something deeper—would you like to explore this instead?
  • What feels most important now?

Close the loop

  • What are you taking from this conversation?
  • What became clearer?
  • Did we work on what mattered most to you?
  • What, if anything, do you want to do with what you now see?
WATCH OUT

Do not confuse the topic with the agreement.

“My manager is difficult,” “I need confidence,” or “my team is not performing” are topics. The agreement is what the client wants from exploring that topic today. Without that distinction, the coach can spend a whole session solving the wrong problem beautifully.

Relationship agreement vs session agreement

Relationship / engagement agreement

Defines the broader coaching container: roles, confidentiality, sponsor expectations, logistics, technology, notes, boundaries, review and closure. Use the Coaching Agreements area for this.

Session agreement

Defines what this particular conversation needs to support. It is lighter, immediate and revisited whenever the client’s purpose changes during the session.
