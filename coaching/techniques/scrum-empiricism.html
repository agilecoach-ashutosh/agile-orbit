Scrum Empiricism — Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation

Scrum is founded on empiricism: make relevant reality transparent, inspect it frequently, and adapt when evidence shows a change is needed.

PROCESSEMPIRICISM & FEEDBACK LOOPSRESEARCHED METHOD
← Back to Empiricism & Feedback LoopsUnderstand the method ↓
Core idea: Turn Scrum events and metrics into decision loops instead of status-reporting rituals.
TRANSPARENCY→→ INSPECTION→→ ADAPTATION

Understand the method first

Transparency

The emergent process and work are visible enough that people can make decisions from a shared basis.

Inspection

Artifacts and progress toward goals are inspected frequently enough to detect important variance or problems.

Adaptation

When the evidence shows that process, product or plan is outside acceptable limits, people adjust as soon as practical.

Lineage: The Scrum Guide identifies Transparency, Inspection and Adaptation as the three empirical Scrum pillars.

How to use it

  1. Choose the decision that needs better evidence.
  2. Make the relevant work, outcome or risk transparent.
  3. Inspect at a cadence appropriate to uncertainty.
  4. Decide what the evidence means.
  5. Adapt product, plan, process or behaviour—and make the next decision visible.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What is currently hidden that makes this decision weak?”

“Are we inspecting something that nobody is empowered to change?”

“What did we adapt because of the last review?”

“Which feedback loop is too slow to influence the decision?”

Agile example

A Sprint Review shows low adoption of a released capability. Instead of declaring the feature “done,” the Product Owner changes the hypothesis and backlog based on what was learned.

Watch for

Watch for

Inspection without adaptation is reporting, not empiricism.

Watch for

Transparency is not exposing people for blame; it is making the system and evidence visible.
