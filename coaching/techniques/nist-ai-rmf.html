NIST AI Risk Management Framework

A voluntary framework for managing AI risk through the functions Govern, Map, Measure and Manage, with trustworthiness considered across the AI lifecycle.

Core idea: Give Agile Coaches and teams a credible structure for discussing AI risk, accountability, human oversight and evidence without inventing ad-hoc controls.
GOVERN→MAP→MEASURE→MANAGE

Understand the method first

Govern

Establish policies, accountability, roles, culture and risk-management processes.

Map

Understand the context, intended use, affected people, capabilities and potential impacts.

Measure

Assess, test and monitor relevant risks and trustworthiness characteristics.

Manage

Prioritise, respond to, monitor and communicate AI risks over time.

Lineage: Published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) as AI RMF 1.0, with a Generative AI Profile and playbook guidance.

How to use it

  1. Define the AI use case, context and consequence before choosing controls.
  2. Clarify human roles and accountability for decisions and oversight.
  3. Map data, permissions, affected stakeholders and failure modes.
  4. Define evidence, tests and monitoring proportionate to risk.
  5. Set escalation, stop and rollback conditions before granting consequential autonomy.
  6. Review risk as the model, tool access or operating context changes.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What can this AI system do, and what can it affect?”

“Where must a human remain accountable rather than merely click approve?”

“What evidence makes the output trustworthy enough for this use case?”

“How do we stop, contain or reverse harmful actions?”

Agile example

A coding agent may create a pull request autonomously, but production deployment remains gated by tested evidence and accountable human approval because the consequence is higher.

Watch for

Watch for

A ceremonial human approval is not meaningful oversight.

Watch for

Risk controls should be proportionate; not every AI use case needs the same governance intensity.
