Opportunity Solution Tree

A visual structure connecting a desired outcome to customer opportunities, candidate solutions and assumption tests.

Understand the method first

Desired Outcome

The measurable business or product outcome that gives discovery a clear direction.

Opportunity Space

Customer needs, pain points and desires that could influence the outcome.

Solution Space

Multiple possible solutions for a selected opportunity.

Assumption Tests

Experiments that test whether candidate solutions are desirable, usable, feasible and viable enough to pursue.

Lineage: Teresa Torres developed the Opportunity Solution Tree as part of continuous product discovery practice.

How to use it

  1. Place one clear desired outcome at the top.
  2. Synthesize customer evidence into an opportunity space without inventing needs.
  3. Choose a target opportunity rather than trying to solve the whole tree.
  4. Generate multiple solution options.
  5. Identify and test the riskiest assumptions across the options.
  6. Use evidence to refine, discard or select a solution.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What outcome are we trying to move?”

“Which customer need or pain point is most connected to that outcome?”

“What other solutions could address the same opportunity?”

“What assumption could invalidate this solution fastest?”

Agile example

A product team targeting lower onboarding abandonment maps user confusion and trust concerns as opportunities before deciding whether the answer is better copy, pre-filled data or a redesigned workflow.

Watch for

Watch for

Do not populate the opportunity space from internal brainstorming alone; it should be grounded in customer evidence.

Watch for

The tree is a learning map, not a fixed roadmap.
