Four Types of Resistors

Mike Cohn’s Agile-transition lens differentiates resistance using two dimensions: active vs passive resistance and liking the status quo vs disliking Agile/Scrum.

Core idea: Explore why resistance occurs instead of treating all resistance as the same problem.

Understand the method first

Likes status quo · Passive

Follower

Hopes the change passes or quietly continues old habits without actively fighting it.

Likes status quo · Active

Diehard

Actively protects the existing system, role, status or way of working.

Dislikes Agile/Scrum · Passive

Skeptic

Questions the approach and may resist quietly because they doubt the method or its fit.

Dislikes Agile/Scrum · Active

Saboteur

Actively undermines the transition or attempts to make it fail.

Lineage: Mike Cohn published the model through Mountain Goat Software and Succeeding with Agile. The four labels are Followers, Diehards, Skeptics and Saboteurs.

How to use it

  1. Observe behaviour without immediately applying a label.
  2. Ask whether the concern is about losing the current state or disliking the proposed Agile change.
  3. Notice whether resistance is active or passive.
  4. Listen for evidence, history, incentives, identity and previous change experience.
  5. Choose an engagement approach based on the cause, not the label.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What are you worried will be lost if this change succeeds?”

“What evidence makes you doubt this approach?”

“What happened in previous transformations that shapes your reaction?”

“What would increase confidence enough to try a small experiment?”

Agile example

A senior analyst challenges Scrum because earlier “Agile” transformations removed useful analysis time. Treating them as a Skeptic invites evidence and experimentation instead of dismissing them as negative.

Watch for

Watch for

Never turn the model into a list of “bad people.”

Watch for

Resistance is information; the change itself may be poorly designed or poorly explained.
