Growth Mindset

A belief that human capabilities can develop over time rather than being permanently fixed.

PEOPLEMINDSET & LEARNING CULTURERESEARCHED METHOD
← Back to Mindset & Learning CultureUnderstand the method ↓
Core idea: Shift coaching conversations from proving competence toward learning, practice, strategy and feedback.

Understand the method first

Fixed mindset

Capability is treated as largely fixed. Failure can feel like evidence about identity or talent.

Growth mindset

Capability is treated as developable through learning, strategies, feedback, practice and support.

Lineage: Carol Dweck’s research at Stanford distinguishes fixed beliefs about ability from growth beliefs that capability can develop. Research also shows effects vary by person and context.

How to use it

  1. Notice fixed language such as “we are just bad at this” or “that person is not technical.”
  2. Separate current capability from permanent identity.
  3. Define the capability that needs to grow and the conditions required for learning.
  4. Choose a small practice, feedback loop or coaching experiment.
  5. Review learning and changed capability, not only whether the first attempt succeeded.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What skill or capability could be developed here?”

“What strategy have we not tried yet?”

“What feedback would help us learn faster?”

“What part of this problem is structural rather than a mindset issue?”

Agile example

Instead of concluding “the team cannot estimate,” a coach asks what uncertainty they struggle to reason about and designs small forecasting practice around real work.

Watch for

Watch for

Growth mindset is not “just try harder.” Strategy, context, opportunity and support matter.

Watch for

Do not use mindset language to hide structural problems such as overload, poor incentives or missing authority.
