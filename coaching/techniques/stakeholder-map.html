Stakeholder Map

Map stakeholders by their stake in the product and their influence over it, then choose an intentional engagement strategy.

Core idea: Focus attention on people who materially affect, fund, use, enable or constrain the product instead of treating every opinion as equal.
Identify→Map Stake × Influence→Notice patterns→Choose engagement→Revisit

Understand the method first

High stake · High influence

Promoters

People with meaningful skin in the game and strong influence. Involve them deeply in learning, decisions and feedback.

High stake · Lower influence

Defenders

People strongly affected by the product but with less power over its direction. Keep them close to real feedback and usage.

Lower stake · High influence

Latents

People with power over the product but limited direct stake. Keep them appropriately satisfied without letting approval chains dominate value decisions.

Lower stake · Lower influence

Audience

People who may need information but usually do not require intensive involvement.

Lineage: The Liberators describe this Stakeholder Map as a practice they adapted for Scrum Teams. Its two axes are Stake and Influence, with the quadrants Promoters, Defenders, Latents and Audience.

How to use it

  1. List people or groups who have real skin in the game or meaningful influence over the product.
  2. Place each stakeholder on the Stake × Influence map based on current context.
  3. Look for patterns: too many Latents can indicate approval-heavy governance; too few Defenders can indicate weak user involvement.
  4. Decide how each group should be involved: collaborate, consult, keep close, or inform.
  5. Revisit the map when product boundaries, leadership, regulation or decision rights change.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“Who experiences consequences when this product succeeds or fails?”

“Who can materially redirect, approve or block the work?”

“Who are we treating as a stakeholder even though they have little stake in the outcome?”

“Which stakeholder needs to be involved earlier so feedback arrives before commitment?”

Agile example

A regulated onboarding team maps Compliance as a Promoter rather than waiting for final approval. Compliance joins discovery and refinement early, reducing late rework without taking over Product Owner accountability.

Watch for

Watch for

Do not turn the quadrants into permanent labels; stakeholder position changes with context.

Watch for

Influence is not the same as seniority, and stake is not the same as having an opinion.
