Lean Startup MVP & Build-Measure-Learn

Use the smallest credible product experiment to maximise validated learning, then iterate through Build → Measure → Learn.

Core idea: Reduce uncertainty before committing large amounts of product and engineering effort.
Build→Measure→Learn→→ next experiment

Understand the method first

Minimum

Only enough scope to test the important learning question—not a thin slice of every planned feature.

Viable

Credible enough for the intended learning context and safe enough for real participants.

Product / experiment

The vehicle for learning can be software, a prototype, a concierge/manual service, landing page or other test depending on the question.

Lineage: Eric Ries popularised the Minimum Viable Product and Build-Measure-Learn loop through Lean Startup practice.

How to use it

  1. State the riskiest assumption or learning question.
  2. Choose the least expensive credible way to test it.
  3. Define the evidence and decision threshold before running the experiment.
  4. Build or stage the MVP experiment.
  5. Measure behaviour and outcomes, not only opinions.
  6. Learn and decide: persevere, change the experiment, pivot, or stop.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What must be true for this idea to work?”

“What is the riskiest assumption?”

“What is the smallest credible test that could change our decision?”

“What will we do differently if the evidence is negative?”

Agile example

Before automating an advisory workflow, the team runs a controlled concierge version with a small user group to learn whether people understand and trust the recommendation.

Watch for

Watch for

MVP does not mean “poor-quality first release.”

Watch for

Do not build a large “MVP” that tests many assumptions at once and produces ambiguous learning.
