Evidence-Based Management (EBM)

A Scrum.org framework for improving value delivery under uncertainty through goals, evidence and experimentation.

Core idea: Move product conversations from output volume toward customer outcomes and the organisation’s ability to deliver value.

Understand the method first

Current Value (CV)

The value customers and stakeholders experience from the product today.

Unrealized Value (UV)

Potential future value that could be realised by meeting unmet needs or opportunities.

Time-to-Market (T2M)

How quickly the organisation can deliver value and learn from feedback.

Ability-to-Innovate (A2I)

How effectively the organisation can deliver new capabilities and remove impediments to innovation.

Lineage: Evidence-Based Management was collaboratively developed by Scrum.org, the Professional Scrum Trainer community, Ken Schwaber and Christina Schwaber.

How to use it

  1. Clarify the strategic or product goal.
  2. Identify evidence that describes customer outcomes—not only activity and output.
  3. Use the four Key Value Areas as lenses to find improvement opportunities.
  4. Form a hypothesis about what change might move the outcome.
  5. Run an experiment, inspect evidence and adapt the goal, product or operating system.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What customer outcome are we trying to improve?”

“Which measure tells us about value rather than activity?”

“What do the four KVAs reveal that our delivery metrics hide?”

“What evidence would cause us to change direction?”

Agile example

A team shipping more features sees no improvement in customer retention. EBM reframes success around Current Value and Time-to-Market, then uses experiments to learn which outcomes actually matter.

Watch for

Watch for

EBM does not prescribe one universal metric set. Measures are context-specific.

Watch for

Do not turn the four KVAs into another dashboard compliance exercise.
