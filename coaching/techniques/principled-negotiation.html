Principled Negotiation

Resolve disagreement by separating people from the problem, focusing on interests rather than positions, creating options for mutual gain, and using objective criteria.

Core idea: Move a team beyond positional bargaining when both sides are protecting legitimate needs.
People ≠ problem→Interests > positions→Create options→Use objective criteria

Understand the method first

Separate people from the problem

Address emotion, relationship and perception without turning the other person into the problem.

Focus on interests, not positions

Ask why the stated demand matters. Interests reveal needs, risks, fears and outcomes that may allow more options.

Invent options for mutual gain

Generate possibilities before deciding. Look for ways to satisfy important interests on both sides.

Use objective criteria

Where interests still conflict, use standards, evidence, precedent or agreed decision criteria rather than power alone.

Lineage: Developed by Roger Fisher, William Ury and Bruce Patton through the Harvard Negotiation Project and described in Getting to Yes.

How to use it

  1. State each side’s current position without attacking it.
  2. Ask what need, risk or outcome the position is trying to protect.
  3. Reframe the conflict as a shared problem containing both sets of interests.
  4. Generate multiple options before evaluating them.
  5. Use agreed evidence or objective criteria to choose when trade-offs remain.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What important concern sits underneath your preferred solution?”

“What is the other side trying to protect?”

“If neither current position existed, what other options could satisfy the interests?”

“What fair or objective criteria could help us decide?”

Agile example

Product says “release Friday”; Engineering says “we need two weeks.” Interests reveal a regulatory deadline on one side and reliability risk on the other. The team explores a thinner compliant slice, targeted controls and a follow-on release instead of arguing only over dates.

Watch for

Watch for

Principled negotiation does not guarantee a win-win outcome; genuine trade-offs can remain.

Watch for

Do not use “interests” to pressure someone into revealing personal information they do not need to share.
