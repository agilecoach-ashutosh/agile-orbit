Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF)

A SAFe prioritisation model that sequences work using relative Cost of Delay divided by relative job duration/size.

PRODUCTPRIORITISATION & TRADE-OFFSRESEARCHED METHOD
← Back to Prioritisation & Trade-offsUnderstand the method ↓
Core idea: Make economic trade-offs explicit when multiple pieces of work compete for limited capacity.
Formula: WSJF = Relative Cost of Delay ÷ Relative Job Duration / Size

Understand the method first

User / Business Value

Relative value to customers or the business.

Time Criticality

How strongly the cost changes if the work is delayed.

Risk Reduction / Opportunity Enablement

Relative contribution to reducing risk or enabling future value.

Job Duration / Size

Relative effort or duration used as the denominator.

Lineage: SAFe applies WSJF using relative Cost of Delay and job duration/size. Its Cost of Delay framing draws on Don Reinertsen’s product-development-flow work.

How to use it

  1. Bring the competing items into one comparison set.
  2. Estimate the Cost of Delay components relatively, not as false financial precision.
  3. Estimate relative job duration or size.
  4. Calculate WSJF and order from highest to lowest.
  5. Challenge the ranking with context, dependencies, hard deadlines and evidence before committing.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What gets more expensive or less valuable if we delay this?”

“Are we comparing items on the same relative scale?”

“Is the job-size estimate hiding uncertainty?”

“What assumption in the ranking would most change the decision?”

Agile example

A smaller regulatory-risk item can rank above a large feature because its delay cost is high and job size is modest.

Watch for

Watch for

WSJF is decision support, not objective truth.

Watch for

Do not use the number to hide strategic judgment or pretend uncertain estimates are precise.
