Prosci ADKAR Model

A model of individual change built around Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability and Reinforcement.

PROCESSCHANGE & TRANSFORMATION COACHINGRESEARCHED METHOD

Core idea: Diagnose where adoption is stuck instead of responding to every change problem with more communication or training.
Awareness→Desire→Knowledge→Ability→Reinforcement

Understand the method first

Awareness

Understanding why the change is needed—not merely knowing that a change is happening.

Desire

Willingness to participate in and support the change.

Knowledge

Knowing how to change, including information, skills and understanding.

Ability

Being able to demonstrate the new skills and behaviours in real work.

Reinforcement

Conditions that sustain the change so people do not revert when pressure returns.

Lineage: Prosci founder Jeff Hiatt developed ADKAR after studying patterns of organisational change. Prosci defines the five outcomes as Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability and Reinforcement.

How to use it

  1. Describe the specific behaviour or adoption outcome required.
  2. Assess which ADKAR outcome is currently the first meaningful gap.
  3. Choose an intervention that addresses that gap rather than jumping ahead.
  4. Observe evidence of movement in real work.
  5. Reassess because different groups may be at different points.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“Do people understand why this change matters?”

“Do they want to participate—or what is getting in the way of Desire?”

“Do they know how to work differently?”

“Can they actually perform the new behaviour under real conditions?”

“What reinforces the old way of working?”

Agile example

A team understands a new engineering practice and has completed training, but cannot use it because the delivery pipeline lacks access and tooling. The gap is Ability, not Knowledge.

Watch for

Watch for

ADKAR describes change outcomes, not a script for manipulating people.

Watch for

Organisational constraints can block individual change even when motivation is high.
