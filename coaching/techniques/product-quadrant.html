Product Quadrant

A role-clarity lens for seeing product-management work across two dimensions: strategic ↔ operational and external ↔ internal. The point is not to create four new job titles; it is to reveal where important product work is owned, missing or trapped in hand-offs.

Core idea: Product work spans far more than backlog management. Coaching role clarity means making strategy, market/customer work, product discovery, delivery decisions and internal coordination visible—then clarifying who is accountable for which decisions.
This is a practitioner product-management lens, not an official Scrum accountability model. Scrum still defines one Product Owner accountability for maximizing product value.

The four areas of the quadrant

EXTERNAL · STRATEGIC

Market & Product Strategy

Customer problems, market context, product vision, positioning, business outcomes, product lifecycle direction and strategic choices.

  • Who are we serving?
  • What problem/opportunity matters?
  • What outcome should the product create?
INTERNAL · STRATEGIC

Organisational Alignment

Connecting product direction to business strategy, investment choices, capabilities, stakeholder alignment and portfolio constraints.

  • What are we choosing to invest in?
  • What capabilities must exist?
  • Which trade-offs need leadership alignment?
EXTERNAL · OPERATIONAL

Customer & Market Feedback

Ongoing user/customer learning, go-to-market collaboration, feedback, adoption signals and market response.

  • What are users actually experiencing?
  • What evidence changes our next decision?
  • How quickly does feedback reach the team?
INTERNAL · OPERATIONAL

Product Delivery & Backlog

Backlog ordering, clarification, release choices, day-to-day decision support, risk/trade-off conversations and collaboration with Developers.

  • What should we do next?
  • What can we stop?
  • What decision is waiting for permission?

How roles often appear around the quadrant

RoleTypical centre of gravityCoaching caution
Product ManagerOften stronger in market-facing strategy, lifecycle and business outcomes.Do not let strategy disconnect from delivery evidence.
Product OwnerAccountable in Scrum for maximizing product value and effective Product Backlog management.A PO reduced to “ticket manager” usually lacks the context/authority required for value decisions.
Project / Delivery ManagerOften stronger in coordination, schedules, dependencies, delivery risk and execution.Delivery coordination should not silently replace product-value accountability.
Business AnalystOften contributes analysis, discovery, detail, stakeholder clarification and solution understanding.Analysis can support decisions without becoming a proxy for an absent product decision-maker.

How an Agile Coach uses the quadrant

  1. List recurring product activities and decisions—not job titles.
  2. Place each activity where it naturally sits across strategic/operational and external/internal dimensions.
  3. Write who currently decides, who advises and who executes.
  4. Circle activities with no clear accountability or multiple competing decision-makers.
  5. Identify where accountability exists without enough information or authority.
  6. Design one clarification experiment: move a decision, involve a missing role earlier, or remove an unnecessary approval.

Powerful questions

“Which product decisions can the Product Owner make without escalation?”

“Where does product strategy actually enter backlog decisions?”

“Which role currently owns a task but not the decision?”

“What product work is nobody doing because everyone assumes someone else owns it?”

Example

A Product Owner is spending 80% of time writing stories and coordinating delivery but cannot change priority without a Product Manager and steering committee. The quadrant makes the gap visible: operational activity sits with the PO, while strategic context and authority sit elsewhere. The coaching conversation becomes about decision rights and product context—not asking the PO to “be more empowered”.

Watch for

Using the quadrant as an org chart

The model should reveal work and decisions, not prescribe one universal role structure.

Equating Product Owner with Project Manager

The accountabilities come from different disciplines and are not interchangeable simply because both coordinate work.

Backlog-only product ownership

Maximizing value requires enough access to customers, strategy, evidence and decision authority.

More roles = more clarity

Adding titles can increase hand-offs. Clarity comes from explicit decision boundaries and collaboration.
