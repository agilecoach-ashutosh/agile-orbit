DiSC

A behavioural model built around Dominance, influence, Steadiness and Conscientiousness.

Core idea: Give teams a shared language for discussing communication preferences without assuming one style is better than another.

Understand the method first

Results · challenge · directness

D — Dominance

Often associated with confidence, pace, challenge and a focus on results. Communication may work better when it is concise and outcome-oriented.

Relationships · enthusiasm · persuasion

i — influence

Often associated with openness, energy, social interaction and influencing others. Conversation and visible enthusiasm may matter.

Cooperation · stability · sincerity

S — Steadiness

Often associated with patience, support, consistency and cooperation. Sudden change or unnecessarily confrontational communication can be harder.

Quality · accuracy · expertise

C — Conscientiousness

Often associated with analysis, accuracy, evidence and quality. Detail, logic and time to process can improve communication.

Lineage: The Everything DiSC model is produced by Wiley and traces its conceptual roots to William Moulton Marston. Agile Orbit explains the four behavioural styles; it does not reproduce any proprietary assessment.

How to use it

  1. Use observed behaviour as a hypothesis, not a diagnosis of personality.
  2. Notice pace, directness, social orientation, stability and appetite for detail.
  3. Adapt the structure of your communication to the person and the situation.
  4. Ask directly what would help them make the decision or engage effectively.
  5. Treat blended styles and context as normal; avoid forcing people into one box.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“What communication pattern helps this person engage without losing the message?”

“Am I adapting to the person—or stereotyping them?”

“What evidence tells me my assumption about their preference is correct?”

“How might stress or context change the behaviour I am seeing?”

Agile example

For a stakeholder who consistently asks for evidence, risks and detail before deciding, the coach helps the team lead with decision criteria and facts instead of interpreting the behaviour as resistance.

Watch for

Watch for

DiSC describes behavioural tendencies, not intelligence, capability or moral character.

Watch for

Do not use an informal guess as though it were a formal assessment result.
