Five Levels of Conflict

A conflict-intensity model that helps a coach recognise when a disagreement is still about solving a problem—and when it has escalated into winning, group protection or destructive opposition.

Core idea: The higher the conflict level, the less useful ordinary problem-solving techniques become. A coach first needs to recognise the intensity, then choose an intervention that matches it.

The model originates with conflict work by Speed B. Leas and is widely used in Agile coaching literature. Agile adaptations commonly use the labels below: Problem to Solve → Disagreement → Contest → Crusade → World War.

Do not treat this as a diagnostic label for people. It is a lens for the interaction. A team can move up or down the levels, and different people may experience the same conflict at different levels.

The five levels — as the conflict escalates

LEVEL 1Problem to Solve

Problem to Solve

The conflict is still primarily about the issue. People may disagree strongly, but they remain able to exchange information and work on the problem together.

MAIN AIMFix the problem
LANGUAGEOpen, specific, fact-based
ATMOSPHEREConstructive tension; optimism that the issue can be solved

What the coach may hear

“I see it differently.” · “What evidence do we have?” · “Can we try both options?” · “I understand your point, but I prefer another approach.”

Best coaching response

  • Facilitate direct conversation and shared facts.
  • Clarify the problem, decision and constraints.
  • Encourage healthy disagreement rather than suppressing it.
  • Use collaborative problem solving, experiments or a clear decision rule.
LEVEL 2Disagreement

Disagreement

Self-protection starts becoming as important as solving the issue. People become more cautious about what they say, and interpretations begin to mix with facts.

MAIN AIMSolve the issue without being hurt or losing position
LANGUAGEGuarded, less specific, increasingly open to interpretation
ATMOSPHERECaution, uncertainty, reduced trust

What the coach may hear

“We tried their idea before.” · “I don't think they understand.” · “I need to be careful how I say this.” · Side conversations begin to replace direct dialogue.

Best coaching response

  • Bring the conversation back to observable facts and current events.
  • Separate what was observed from what is being assumed.
  • Surface interests, concerns and fears without forcing quick agreement.
  • Restore direct dialogue before coalitions and stories harden.
LEVEL 3Contest

Contest

Winning begins to matter more than resolving. Positions harden, factions may form, and communication becomes distorted by over-generalisation, blame and personal attacks.

MAIN AIMWin
LANGUAGECombative, exaggerated, personal
ATMOSPHEREFrustration, resentment, us-versus-them

What the coach may hear

“They always do this.” · “If only that person wasn't involved.” · “Everyone agrees with me.” · “Their team is the problem.”

Best coaching response

  • Slow or pause the direct debate if people are reinforcing win/lose behaviour.
  • Re-establish boundaries for respectful interaction.
  • Work with facts separately from accusations and historical grievances.
  • Use mediation, smaller conversations or structured facilitation before returning to joint problem solving.
LEVEL 4Crusade

Crusade

The conflict becomes ideological. Protecting one's own group or identity becomes central, and the other side is increasingly seen as fundamentally wrong, untrustworthy or something that should be removed.

MAIN AIMProtect our side; defeat or remove the opposition
LANGUAGERighteous, punitive, ideological
ATMOSPHEREAlienation, hostility, entrenched camps

What the coach may hear

“There is no point talking to them.” · “People like that never change.” · “Our group needs protection from theirs.”

Best coaching response

  • Do not run a normal team workshop as though this were Level 1.
  • Prioritise containment, safety, clear behavioural boundaries and leadership support.
  • Use experienced mediation or separate conversations where direct interaction is no longer constructive.
  • Work first on reducing hostility and restoring enough safety for communication.
LEVEL 5World War

World War

The objective has become destruction or total defeat of the other side. Communication may be openly hostile or may stop almost completely. The original issue can become secondary to the conflict itself.

MAIN AIMDestroy or eliminate the opposition
LANGUAGEHostile, retaliatory—or no direct communication
ATMOSPHEREHopelessness, revenge, severe breakdown

What the coach may observe

Direct contact has broken down. People act through escalation channels, formal complaints, political alliances or retaliatory behaviour. Safety and organisational risk may now be involved.

Best coaching response

  • Stop treating this as a standard Agile-team facilitation problem.
  • Separate parties where necessary to prevent further damage.
  • Escalate to appropriate organisational leadership, HR, employee-relations or professional conflict support depending on context.
  • Focus first on safety, containment and clear boundaries—not forced reconciliation.

LevelPrimary focusLanguageCoach emphasis

How an Agile Coach can use the model

  1. Listen to the language being used—not just the topic being argued about.
  2. Observe whether people are solving, protecting, winning, defending a camp or trying to eliminate the other side.
  3. Estimate the current level conservatively; do not dramatise ordinary disagreement.
  4. Choose an intervention appropriate to the level.
  5. De-escalate one step at a time instead of trying to jump straight from severe conflict to collaboration.
  6. Escalate beyond coaching when safety, harassment, legal, employee-relations or serious organisational risk is involved.
Key coaching principle: techniques that work beautifully at Level 1 can make Level 4 or 5 worse. The intervention must match the intensity.

Agile team example

LEVEL 1

Architecture choice

Two engineers disagree on a design. They compare trade-offs and run a spike. Healthy conflict.

LEVEL 3

Contest

Weeks later, each side recruits allies and starts framing the other as incompetent. The coach pauses solution debate and works on de-escalation.

LEVEL 4+

Crusade

People refuse direct interaction and demand that the other group be removed. Normal retrospective facilitation is no longer sufficient.

Common mistakes

Calling every disagreement “conflict”.

Level 1 conflict is often exactly what a high-performing team needs to make better decisions.

Forcing collaboration too early.

When people are at a severe level, first reduce hostility and restore enough safety for dialogue.

Labelling individuals.

Say “the interaction looks like Level 3” rather than “this person is Level 3”.

Trying to coach beyond your remit.

Serious threats, harassment, retaliation or formal employee issues need appropriate organisational/professional handling.

Model lineage: Speed B. Leas's conflict-level framework, later adapted and popularised for Agile coaching by Lyssa Adkins. Agile Orbit uses the five Agile-coaching labels: Problem to Solve, Disagreement, Contest, Crusade and World War.
