Theory of Constraints — Five Focusing Steps

Improve the whole system by identifying the constraint that currently limits throughput and focusing improvement there.

PROCESSFLOW & BOTTLENECK COACHINGRESEARCHED METHOD
← Back to Flow & Bottleneck CoachingUnderstand the method ↓
Core idea: Prevent local optimisation that makes people busier without improving end-to-end flow.
Identify→Exploit→Subordinate→Elevate→Repeat

Understand the method first

Identify

Find the current system constraint—the factor limiting the rate at which the system reaches its goal.

Exploit

Use the existing constraint as effectively as possible before adding major investment.

Subordinate

Align the rest of the system to support the constraint rather than optimising locally.

Elevate

Increase the capacity or capability of the constraint if needed.

Repeat

When the constraint moves, start again. Do not let inertia preserve an old improvement focus.

Lineage: Eliyahu M. Goldratt developed the Theory of Constraints. Its Five Focusing Steps are Identify, Exploit, Subordinate, Elevate and Repeat / avoid inertia.

How to use it

  1. Define the system and the flow outcome you care about.
  2. Use queues, waiting and blocked work to identify the likely constraint.
  3. Protect the constraint from avoidable work and interruptions.
  4. Reduce upstream overproduction that only grows the queue.
  5. Invest or redesign only after exploiting and subordinating.
  6. Measure where the constraint moves next.

Coach with it

Use the method as a lens for inquiry, not as a rigid prescription. Useful coaching questions include:

“Where does work consistently wait or accumulate?”

“If this step became faster, would end-to-end flow actually improve?”

“What are upstream teams doing that overloads the constraint?”

“Where did the bottleneck move after our last improvement?”

Agile example

Testing is the constraint. Asking developers to produce more code increases the queue. The team instead limits incoming WIP, helps testing, improves automation and then measures where the new constraint appears.

Watch for

Watch for

The loudest problem is not always the system constraint.

Watch for

Do not keep optimising yesterday’s bottleneck after the constraint has moved.
