The Empty Cup

A simple reminder: expertise becomes unhelpful when it fills the space before the client has had a chance to think.

The teaching story

A visitor comes to a Zen master already full of ideas about what he knows. The master pours tea until the cup overflows.

“Just like this cup, your mind is already full — full of your own opinions, your own conclusions, and your own ‘knowledge.’”

What a “full cup” sounds like in coaching

Interpret too quickly

“This is clearly a confidence issue.”

Solve too quickly

“You need to talk to your manager.”

Label too quickly

“That sounds like imposter syndrome.”

Teach too quickly

“Let me give you a framework for that.”

What an “empty cup” can sound like

Stay with meaning

“When you say confidence, what does confidence mean to you here?”

Notice without deciding

“I noticed your pace changed when you mentioned that. What are you noticing?”

Separate fact from story

“What do you know, and what are you currently interpreting?”

Check your own agenda

“Am I following the client—or trying to prove I can solve this?”

Coach self-check

  • What conclusion am I already holding?
  • What am I hoping the client will realise?
  • What question would I ask if I did not need to be clever?
  • What might I hear if I temporarily set my expertise down?

This page uses the widely circulated “empty cup” Zen teaching story as a coaching metaphor. Treat it as a reflective parable rather than a historical or scientific claim.
