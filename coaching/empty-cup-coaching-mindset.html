The Empty Cup

A short Zen teaching story about what can happen when we arrive already full of our own opinions, conclusions and knowledge.

The story

There was once a famous scholar who believed he understood everything about life and religion.

One day, he went to visit a Zen master to ask for guidance.

As soon as he sat down, the scholar started talking non-stop. He talked about the sutras he had read, the ideas he agreed with, the schools of thought he had studied, and all the things he believed he already knew.

The master simply sat there, listening quietly. He did not interrupt.

After a while, the master gently said, “Let’s have some tea.”

He took a teapot and began to pour tea into the scholar’s cup. The cup became full, but the master kept pouring. Tea spilled over the edge, flowed onto the table, and started dripping onto the floor.

Shocked, the scholar shouted, “Stop! The cup is already full! You can’t pour any more into it!”

“Just like this cup, your mind is already full — full of your own opinions, your own conclusions, and your own ‘knowledge.’”

Reflection for a coach

What do I need to empty—or temporarily set aside—before I can genuinely hear this client?

Note: The Empty Cup is used here as a motivational reflection story. It is not presented as one of the formal coaching mindset principles.
