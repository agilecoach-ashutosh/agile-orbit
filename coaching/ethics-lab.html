CURRENT ICF CODE · PRACTICE LENS

Five areas to keep visible.

The current ICF Code of Ethics took effect on April 1, 2025. These five headings mirror the Code's ethical-standards sections for ICF professionals.

01Client & Sponsor Agreements

Roles, responsibilities, confidentiality, financial arrangements and other engagement expectations are made clear before and during the work.

02Confidentiality & Legal Compliance

Protect information, understand agreed limits, and consider applicable law and technology-related obligations.

03Conduct & Conflicts of Interest

Surface multiple roles, incentives, power and relationships that could affect professional judgment or client trust.

04Consistent Value

Stay alert to whether coaching remains useful, appropriate and within competence rather than continuing by default.

05Integrity & Accountability

Represent yourself accurately, own mistakes, seek ethical guidance and act consistently with professional commitments.

REASONING PATH

Before deciding, slow the dilemma down.

1 · FactsWhat do I actually know, and what am I assuming?
2 · AgreementsWhat was explicitly contracted with client, sponsor and others?
3 · StakeholdersWho may be affected, and whose interests are pulling me?
4 · DutiesWhat confidentiality, law, policy, competence or role obligations apply?
5 · OptionsWhat responses protect agency, trust and transparency?
6 · ConsultDo I need supervision, ethics guidance, legal advice or another qualified professional?

These scenarios are educational prompts, not legal advice or formal ICF adjudications. Local law, organisational policy and the exact coaching agreement may materially change what is appropriate.
RED FLAGS

Signals that deserve a deliberate ethics check.

“They are paying, so they can know.”

Sponsor payment does not automatically define what session information may be disclosed. Return to the agreement.

“It is only for my convenience.”

Recording, AI, cloud notes and third-party tools can create new privacy and consent questions even when the coach's intention is benign.

“I can keep the roles separate in my head.”

A dual role may still create actual or perceived conflict. The client needs enough transparency to make an informed choice.

“The client asked, so I can do it.”

Client preference does not expand professional competence or remove the need to change hats explicitly.

“No one will know.”

Ethical reasoning is not only about detection. Ask whether the action remains defensible if fully transparent to the client and relevant stakeholders.

“I must solve this alone.”

Consultation, supervision, specialist advice and referral are signs of professional judgment, not failure.

Professional reference

The current ICF Code of Ethics took effect on April 1, 2025 and is designed to support ethical reflection and decision-making in complex situations. It includes explicit attention to client/sponsor agreements, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, technology systems and multiple professional roles.
