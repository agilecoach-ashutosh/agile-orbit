Coaching Ethics Lab

Ethics is rarely a tidy multiple-choice problem. Work through realistic tensions, identify the agreements and stakeholders involved, and practise ethical reasoning rather than memorising rules.

Ethical dilemma
Reasoning compass

Before deciding, ask:

What was agreed?Relationship, session, confidentiality, sponsor expectations, technology and boundaries.
Who are the stakeholders?Client, sponsor, organisation, coach and any other affected party.
Whose interests are pulling me?Notice conflicts, incentives, dual roles and pressure.
What preserves client autonomy and trust?Be transparent about limits rather than quietly deciding on the client's behalf.
Do I need consultation or referral?Ethical maturity includes recognising limits and seeking appropriate support.
These scenarios are learning prompts, not legal advice or formal ICF adjudications. Local law, organisational policy and the exact coaching agreement may materially change the response.
ETHICS IN PRACTICE

Patterns worth learning to recognise

Confidentiality is contracted

Especially in sponsored coaching, define what may be shared, with whom, for what purpose, and where the limits are.

Technology changes the risk

Recordings, transcripts, AI tools and cloud notes introduce consent, privacy, access and retention questions that should be explicit.

Competence has edges

A coach does not become a therapist, lawyer, doctor or financial adviser simply because the client brings a related issue.
