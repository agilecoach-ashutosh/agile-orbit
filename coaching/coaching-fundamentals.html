What are we actually doing when we coach?

Before models, questions or tools, be clear about the job: create a thinking partnership that increases awareness, choice and client ownership without quietly turning the conversation into advice.

The purpose

Create better thinking

Coaching gives the client enough attention, challenge and reflection to see their situation, patterns and choices more clearly. The coach contributes process, presence and perspective—not a predetermined answer.

The stance

Work with a capable person

Approach the client as someone who can think, choose, learn and act. This does not mean every client already has every piece of information; it means the coach does not assume ownership of the client’s life or decision.

The shift

From fixing → enabling

A coach may notice patterns, offer observations and challenge assumptions, but the aim is to expand the client’s capacity to think rather than create dependence on the coach’s solutions.

The result

Awareness → choice → learning

Action can matter, but action is not the only useful outcome. A conversation may create clarity, a reframe, a decision, a new experiment, or a more honest understanding of what the client wants.

Coaching and adjacent helping roles

The same professional may sometimes use more than one role. The discipline is to know which role is active and make a role change explicit rather than disguising advice as coaching.

CoachingHelps the client think, notice, choose and learn.
MentoringDraws on experience to guide someone through a domain or role.
ConsultingProvides specialist analysis, recommendations or solutions.
TeachingBuilds knowledge or skill through explanation and instruction.
TherapyAddresses needs requiring appropriately qualified mental-health care.

Who owns what?

Coach owns

The quality of the process

  • presence, attention and curiosity;
  • clear contracting and confidentiality;
  • questions, reflections and observations offered with care;
  • professional boundaries and ethical judgment;
  • noticing when another role or support may be needed.
Client owns

The life, meaning and decisions

  • what matters enough to bring;
  • what an experience means to them;
  • whether an observation fits;
  • what choice or experiment they want to make;
  • the consequences of their decisions and actions.
A useful self-check

Am I helping this person think—or helping myself feel useful?

The urge to diagnose, reassure, teach or solve can come from good intention. Coaching skill includes noticing that impulse and choosing whether the client needs exploration, explicit expertise, or a different kind of support.
