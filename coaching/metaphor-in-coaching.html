Metaphor in Coaching

When a client says “I am on a treadmill” or “I have hit a wall,” the metaphor may contain more useful information than the coach's interpretation of it.

Client

“I feel like I am running on a treadmill.”

Coach interpretation

“So you are exhausted?” — maybe, but the coach has replaced the client's image with their own meaning.

Stay inside the client's metaphor

  • What is that treadmill like?
  • What keeps it moving?
  • Where are you on it?
  • What happens if it slows down?
  • What would stepping off mean?
  • Is there anything else about that treadmill?

Mini practice

“My career has hit a wall.”

Try: “What kind of wall is it?” or “What is on the other side, if anything?” Avoid deciding that the wall means fear, bureaucracy or lack of confidence.

“I am carrying everyone.”

Try: “What are you carrying?” “How are you carrying it?” “What happens if you put one thing down?”

“I am at a crossroads.”

Try: “What roads can you see?” “What is different about each?” “Do you have to choose now?”

“It feels foggy.”

Try: “What can you see through the fog?” “What would a little more visibility give you?”

Coach watch-out

The discipline is not to become clever with metaphors. It is to preserve the client's language long enough for the client to discover what their own image contains.
