COACH THE WHOLE SYSTEM

Agile Coaching

Agile coaching supports individuals, teams and organisations in using agile ways of working to achieve better business outcomes. The coach does not merely improve ceremonies—the coach helps the system notice how people collaborate, how product decisions are made, how technology enables change, and how work flows.

ICAgile-aligned framing

ICAgile describes Agile Coaching as supporting people, teams and organisations in effectively applying agile ways of working to achieve business outcomes. Its core coaching skills include professional coaching, facilitation, mentoring and teaching.

ICF coaching foundation

ICF frames coaching as a partnership that stimulates thinking and creativity so clients can maximise their personal and professional potential. In Agile Coaching, that partnership is combined with agile and systems expertise.

AGILE ORBIT LENS

People · Product · Technology · Process

A useful Agile Coach looks beyond the visible symptom. A missed Sprint Goal may be a product-priority problem, an architecture dependency, an approval queue, a capability gap or a conversation the team is avoiding. These four areas give us a practical lens for coaching the whole system.

The coach does not own all four areas. The coach helps the people who do own them see interactions, constraints and feedback loops—then increases the system's ability to improve without dependence on the coach.

01 · PEOPLE

Create the conditions for people to think, speak and act together

Agility is social before it is procedural. Coaches help make relationships, decision patterns, conflict, safety, capability and resistance discussable—without reducing people to labels or treating every human problem as a process problem.

Swipe or use arrows to explore
PEOPLE 01Workbook + linked source

Stakeholder ecosystem

Move beyond a stakeholder list. Identify who has a real stake in the outcome, map influence and involvement, then choose an engagement strategy rather than communicating with everyone in the same way.

Coach forClear decision rights, fewer surprise approvals and earlier involvement of people whose knowledge or authority matters.
AskWhose perspective can change this decision? Who can stop the work? Who lives with the consequence?
Watch forStakeholder maps that become static artefacts instead of a guide for real conversations.

PEOPLE 02Workbook + linked source

Communication awareness, not personality boxes

DiSC can provide language for adapting communication—Dominance, influence, Steadiness and Conscientiousness—but it should create curiosity, not stereotypes. People are more complex than a four-letter model.

Coach forFlexing detail, pace, challenge and relationship-building to the person and situation.
AskWhat does this stakeholder need in order to understand, trust and act on the message?
Watch for“They are a D, so they always…”—a model becoming a shortcut around listening.

PEOPLE 03Added research

Team culture & psychological safety

Google's team-effectiveness research highlighted psychological safety, dependability, structure and clarity, meaning and impact. The coaching task is not to manufacture “happiness”; it is to create conditions where people can raise risks, ask questions and challenge ideas without interpersonal punishment.

Coach forSpeak-up behaviour, dependable commitments, clear decisions and meaningful connection to outcomes.
AskWhat happens here when someone says “I don't know”, disagrees, or admits a mistake?
Watch forPoliteness being mistaken for safety; healthy teams can disagree directly.
Google re:Work ↗
PEOPLE 04Workbook + research

Mindset becomes culture

Your repeated beliefs, behaviours and tolerated norms eventually become “how things work here.” Research on organisational mindsets links perceived fixed-mindset cultures with lower collaboration, innovation, trust and commitment than growth-mindset cultures.

Coach forLearning goals, experimentation, feedback and language that separates a failed experiment from a failed person.
AskWhat do people learn here from a mistake: hide it, explain it, or improve the system?
Watch for“Growth mindset” being used to excuse poor systems or endless extra effort.
Stanford research profile ↗
PEOPLE 05Workbook + research

Conflict is useful data

Thomas-Kilmann describes five conflict-handling modes—competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding and accommodating—across assertiveness and cooperativeness. Coaching is about increasing choice, not declaring one mode universally “Agile”.

Coach forSurfacing differences early and choosing a response that fits the stakes, relationship, time and complexity.
AskWhat are we protecting? What matters to each side? What mode are we overusing by habit?
Watch forForced collaboration when a quick decision, temporary accommodation or deliberate pause is more appropriate.
TKI overview ↗
PEOPLE 06Workbook + linked research

Resistance & change fatigue

Resistance has different causes: fear, experience, loss of status, distrust, overload or a legitimate objection. Change saturation occurs when disruption exceeds people's capacity to absorb it. Treat resistance as information before treating it as a problem.

Coach forUnderstanding the history behind resistance, reducing unnecessary disruption and building change capacity.
AskWhat has this team experienced before? What are they afraid of losing? What capacity do they actually have for one more change?
Watch forLabelling skeptics as “not Agile” instead of learning what their resistance is telling you.

02 · PRODUCT

Move from activity to valuable outcomes

Product coaching helps teams and leaders get clearer about the product boundary, customer problem, decision rights, desired outcomes and evidence. Shipping more is not the goal; learning whether the product creates value is.

Swipe or use arrows to explore
PRODUCT 01Workbook + official source

Define the product boundary

The Scrum Guide describes a product as a vehicle to deliver value, with a clear boundary, known stakeholders and defined users or customers. A product can be a service, physical product or something more abstract—not only something directly sold for revenue.

Coach forA shared understanding of product, users, value and boundaries before optimising the backlog.
AskWhat is the product? Who experiences its value? Where does this product begin and end?
Watch forCalling every component, project or team backlog a product.
Scrum Guide ↗
PRODUCT 02Workbook + linked model

Product role clarity

Product Manager, Product Owner, Business Analyst and Project Manager may coexist, but blurred decision rights create queues and politics. Use role models as conversation starters, then make the local accountabilities explicit.

Coach forClear ownership of product direction, value decisions, discovery, backlog decisions and delivery coordination.
AskWho can say “no”? Who decides value trade-offs? Who owns the outcome after delivery?
Watch forA Product Owner who is accountable for value but lacks authority to make product decisions.
Product role model ↗
PRODUCT 03Workbook + EBM research

Tasks → outputs → outcomes → value

Evidence-Based Management distinguishes activities from outputs and customer outcomes. Completing more tasks or releasing more features can increase activity without improving the customer's experience.

ACTIVITY“We completed 40 stories.”
OUTPUT“We released the feature.”
OUTCOME“Onboarding is 25% faster.”
VALUEBetter experience, lower cost or lower risk.
AskIf this feature succeeds, what changes for the customer or user that could not happen before?
Evidence-Based Management ↗
PRODUCT 04Official source + research

Goals, evidence & product decisions

A Product Goal gives direction; evidence tells us whether our choices are moving toward it. Product coaching makes assumptions explicit and turns delivery into a sequence of learning bets rather than a feature factory.

Coach forOne meaningful direction, explicit assumptions, leading evidence and regular product decisions based on what was learned.
AskWhat evidence would make us continue, change direction or stop investing?
Watch forUsing velocity or number of features as proof that product value increased.
EBM Guide ↗
PRODUCT 05Workbook + Lean Startup

MVP = learning, not a tiny product

Eric Ries defines an MVP around gaining the maximum validated learning about customers with the least effort. The point is not “build a cheap version of everything”; it is to test the riskiest assumption with the smallest credible experiment.

Coach forReducing uncertainty before scaling investment.
AskWhat must we learn before spending more? What is the smallest experiment that can produce trustworthy evidence?
Watch forCalling a badly built first release an MVP while learning nothing specific.
Eric Ries on MVP ↗
PRODUCT 06Workbook case-study theme

Discovery, pivots & business-model learning

A pivot is not random change. It is a deliberate change in direction when evidence weakens the current hypothesis. Kodak is a useful reminder that having technology is not enough if the business model and value proposition fail to adapt.

Coach forSeparating commitment to the customer problem from attachment to the current solution.
AskWhat have we learned that should change our plan? Are we defending sunk cost or pursuing value?
Watch forRoadmaps becoming promises that cannot change even when evidence changes.

03 · TECHNOLOGY

Build systems that make change safe, fast and learnable

Technology Coaching is not the Agile Coach acting as architect or senior developer. It is helping teams and leaders see how technical quality, architecture, automation, AI and engineering capability affect flow, feedback, risk and autonomy.

Swipe or use arrows to explore
TECH 01Added research

Technical excellence enables agility

One of the original Agile principles says continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility. Poor technical health eventually appears as slower learning, fragile releases and a growing cost of change.

Coach forMaking technical debt, refactoring needs, maintainability and quality risks visible in product conversations.
AskWhat part of the system makes a small change unexpectedly hard or risky?
Watch forTrading away quality every Sprint and calling the resulting slowdown a team-performance problem.
Agile principles ↗
TECH 02Added research · DORA

Built-in quality & fast feedback

Reliable automated tests, continuous testing, security checks and short feedback loops reduce the distance between introducing a problem and learning about it. Quality works best when it is part of development, not a downstream inspection phase.

Coach forFast, trusted feedback from code change to quality signal.
AskHow quickly do we learn that a change is unsafe? Where does defect information arrive too late?
Watch forA separate “quality phase” becoming the queue that all fast development eventually hits.
DORA continuous delivery ↗
TECH 03Added research · DORA

Architecture for team autonomy

Technical architecture and team design are connected. DORA highlights loosely coupled teams and services that can test and deploy independently without fine-grained coordination with other teams.

Coach forReducing architecture-driven waiting and making ownership boundaries clearer.
AskWhat changes require permission or coordination outside the team? Which of those dependencies are architectural?
Watch for“Cross-functional team” on the org chart while every meaningful change still crosses five component teams.
DORA loosely coupled teams ↗
TECH 04Added research · DORA

Small batches, CI & continuous delivery

Frequent integration, short-lived branches, deployment automation and pervasive security reduce batch size and shorten feedback. The coaching conversation is less “Are you doing DevOps?” and more “What stops a safe change from flowing to production?”

Coach forSmaller changes, lower integration pain, deployment on demand and security built into the path.
AskWhere does code wait? What requires a release train, freeze, manual hand-off or special hero?
Watch forHigh coding speed with large release batches and slow verification.
DORA capabilities ↗
TECH 052025–26 DORA research

AI-assisted engineering: find where the bottleneck moved

AI can accelerate code creation, but faster creation does not automatically mean faster value delivery. DORA's recent research describes AI as an amplifier and shows that time saved in creation can shift into review, verification and other parts of the SDLC.

IDEA→CODE→REVIEW→TEST→DEPLOY→LEARN
Coaching lens: if AI triples code output but review, testing or release capacity stays fixed, the system may simply create a larger queue.
AskAfter adopting AI, where did waiting increase? What changed in batch size, review load, defects and lead time?
DORA AI research ↗
TECH 06Added research · NIST

Human-in-the-loop quality & AI governance

AI-generated code, analysis or content remains the team's responsibility. Teams need explicit rules for data exposure, review, testing, traceability and where a human decision is mandatory—especially in regulated domains.

Coach forClear AI-use boundaries, evidence-based controls and review proportional to risk.
AskWhat data may enter the tool? Who validates the output? What evidence is required before it reaches a customer or production?
Watch for“The AI wrote it” becoming an accountability gap.
NIST GenAI profile ↗
TECH 07Added research · OWASP

AI agents & safe autonomy

When AI moves from suggesting to acting—calling tools, changing systems or triggering workflows—permission and autonomy become system-design questions. OWASP highlights excessive agency as a risk when functionality, permissions or autonomy exceed what is necessary.

Coach forLeast privilege, clear action boundaries, human approval for consequential steps and observable agent behaviour.
AskWhat may the agent decide? What may it execute? Which action must a human approve every time?
Watch forGiving an AI agent production-level authority simply because the prototype was convenient.
OWASP Excessive Agency ↗
TECH 08Agile Orbit synthesis

Capability, not hero dependency

“Only one person understands this service” is a flow and resilience risk, not merely a staffing fact. Pairing, ensemble work, internal enablement, documentation and deliberate skill spread reduce dependency on individual heroes.

Coach forKnowledge flow, shared ownership and enough capability inside the team to finish its work.
AskWhat stops us if this specialist is unavailable for two weeks? How could capability move closer to the work?
Watch forCelebrating heroics while repeatedly recreating the conditions that require heroes.

04 · PROCESS

Design shorter feedback loops, not more process

Process should help work become visible, decisions arrive sooner and learning change the plan. Coaches challenge ceremony compliance, hidden queues, transformation overload and communication habits that make the system slower than it needs to be.

Swipe or use arrows to explore
PROCESS 01Agile principles + synthesis

Empiricism over process compliance

A process is useful when it makes reality easier to see and adapt to. Running every ceremony perfectly while customer feedback arrives late, decisions wait in queues and risks remain hidden is not agility.

Coach forTransparency, frequent inspection, meaningful adaptation and a process that serves outcomes.
AskWhat new information did this event produce, and what decision changed because of it?
Watch for“We followed Scrum” becoming a substitute for examining whether the system learned anything.
PROCESS 02Workbook

Change without bulldozing

Transformation is not a rollout of rituals. Blend useful practices into context, pace the change, build capability, start with a slice, show evidence, create change champions and keep feedback open.

Blend, don't bulldozePace before raceBuild capabilityStart with a sliceShow evidenceChange championsFit the contextLearn from failureCelebrate progressVisible leadershipConstant feedback
AskWhat is the smallest change this system can absorb and learn from now?
PROCESS 03Workbook + linked source

Resistance is data

Mike Cohn's resistance model distinguishes different ways people may resist change. The coaching value is not the label—it is discovering the underlying reason and choosing a response that fits.

Coach forUnderstanding before persuasion; evidence before pressure.
AskIs this person protecting something important, challenging the logic, fearing loss, or exhausted by previous change?
Watch forTrying to “move people to the right quadrant” instead of learning from their objection.
Four types of resistors ↗
PROCESS 04Workbook + Prosci

Change capacity & fatigue

Prosci distinguishes change disruption from change capacity: saturation occurs when incoming disruption exceeds the system's capacity to absorb it. More transformation activity can therefore reduce transformation capability.

Coach forSequencing change, reducing collisions, strengthening support and respecting the emotional history of the team.
AskWhat is already changing for this team? What support or recovery capacity is missing?
Watch forLaunching another “Agile initiative” into a team that has no capacity left to absorb it.
Prosci change fatigue ↗
PROCESS 05Workbook · refined

Match the channel to ambiguity & stakes

Email and chat are efficient for updates and documentation. Ambiguous, emotional or high-impact conversations usually benefit from richer, faster feedback. The coaching principle is intentional channel choice—not “face-to-face is always better.”

Coach forAsynchronous communication for information; richer synchronous dialogue for alignment, conflict and difficult decisions.
AskIs this message informational, ambiguous, sensitive or consequential? What feedback do we need while communicating it?
Watch forA conflict escalating through long chat threads because nobody changes the medium.
PROCESS 06Existing Agile Orbit intervention

Flow, constraints & dependencies

“Busy” can hide waiting. Dependencies on skills, architecture, approvals, rights, vendors, environments or other teams create queues and pressure. Make them visible, measure their impact and choose what to control, influence, escalate or consciously manage.

Coach forLess surprise, lower blocked time, fewer hand-offs and evidence for system-level action.
AskWhere does work wait longer than it is worked on? Which dependency repeatedly surprises us?

PROCESS 07Workbook corrected with official history

Agile roots: a compact timeline

History matters because Agile was not invented as a ceremony package. It emerged from attempts to handle complex product and software work with more feedback, collaboration and adaptability.

1986Takeuchi & Nonaka describe a faster, flexible, overlapping product-development approach that later inspires Scrum.
1995Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland formally present Scrum at OOPSLA.
2001Seventeen practitioners meet at Snowbird and publish the Manifesto for Agile Software Development.
Why it mattersFrameworks evolved from learning about complex work—not from a desire to standardise meetings.

CONNECT THE SYSTEM

The four areas are not separate problems

Coaching gets powerful when we stop fixing the first symptom and trace how one part of the system shapes another.

Product → People

A “priority problem” may actually be stakeholders avoiding a difficult trade-off or a Product Owner without sufficient decision authority.

Process → Technology

A release delay may look like governance overhead but be amplified by tightly coupled architecture, manual testing or unsafe deployment practices.

Technology → People

A specialist bottleneck can appear to be a staffing problem while the deeper issue is knowledge concentration and architecture that only one person can safely change.

AI → the whole system

AI may make creation faster, but it also changes review load, skills, risk, governance and workflow. DORA's recent research describes AI as an amplifier of the system around it.

Coach the interaction, not only the component.

Ask where value is delayed, where learning arrives late, where ownership is unclear and where the constraint moves after an improvement. Sustainable agility comes from improving the whole path from idea to evidence—not optimising one local activity.
