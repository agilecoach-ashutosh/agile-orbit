How the coach shows up matters

A coaching mindset is open, curious, flexible and client-centred. These four principles shape the quality of the relationship before any model or technique is used.

Mindset Principle

Unconditional Positive Regard

Hold trust in the client as a whole, capable and resourceful person. Suspend judgement and create room for the client to think, choose and learn without needing to earn the coach's approval.

Mindset Principle

Client Centricity

The client sets the agenda and remains responsible for their own choices. The coach works with what matters to the client rather than steering the conversation toward the coach's preferred answer.

Mindset Principle

Congruence

Bring authenticity and alignment into the relationship. The coach does not need to perform a role; openness, transparency and appropriate self-awareness support a genuine coaching presence.

Mindset Principle

Space for Reflection

Create enough psychological and conversational space for thinking to happen. Silence, pauses and non-interruption can be part of the work rather than gaps that need to be filled.

Three mindset checks before or during a conversation

  • Am I listening to understand, not reply?
  • Am I projecting my agenda, or holding space for theirs?
  • Am I curious or certain?
Training note: Coaching mindset is presented here separately from reflective stories and metaphors. The four principles above are the mindset principles used in the source learning material; stories such as The Empty Cup sit alongside them as reflections, not as additional principles.
