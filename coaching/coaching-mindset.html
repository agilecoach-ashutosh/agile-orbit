How the coach shows up matters

Before models, tools or techniques, coaching starts with how the coach shows up: open, curious, flexible and client-centred.

A coaching mindset is not a script or a checklist. It is a way of being with the client while also managing yourself as a practitioner. The principles below combine a strong relational stance with professional disciplines reflected in contemporary coaching standards.

RELATIONAL STANCE

How I meet the person

PRINCIPLE 01🤝

Unconditional Positive Regard

Trust the person, not just the process.

Hold trust in the client as a whole, capable and resourceful person. Suspend judgement and create room for the client to think, choose and learn without needing to earn the coach's approval.

Relational foundation influenced by humanistic coaching and person-centred practice.
PRINCIPLE 02🎯

Client Centricity

Their agenda. Their choices. Their ownership.

The client sets the direction and remains responsible for their own choices. The coach works with what matters to the client rather than steering the conversation toward the coach's preferred answer.

Aligned with ICF's emphasis on client responsibility, partnership and a client-centred mindset.
PRINCIPLE 03🪞

Congruence

Be present as a person, not a performance.

Bring authenticity and alignment into the relationship. The coach does not need to perform a role; openness, transparency and appropriate self-awareness support a genuine coaching presence.

A relational principle associated with person-centred practice and authentic presence.
PRINCIPLE 04⏸️

Space for Reflection

Do not rush to fill the silence.

Create enough psychological and conversational space for thinking to happen. Silence, pauses and non-interruption can be part of the work rather than gaps that need to be filled.

The aim is not silence itself, but enough space for the client's own thinking to emerge.
PROFESSIONAL DISCIPLINE

How I manage myself as a coach

PRINCIPLE 05🔎

Curiosity over Certainty

I do not need to know the answer.

Stay genuinely interested in how the client sees the situation. Hold hypotheses lightly, ask rather than assume, and remain willing to have your interpretation changed by what the client discovers.

ICF describes coaching mindset as open, curious, flexible and client-centred.
PRINCIPLE 06🧭

Self-Awareness & Self-Management

Notice what is happening in me without making it about me.

Notice assumptions, emotional reactions, preferences, intuition and bias. Manage your own state so these signals can inform awareness without quietly taking control of the client's conversation.

ICF explicitly includes awareness of bias, context, culture, emotions, intuition and the coach's influence.
PRINCIPLE 07♻️

Reflective Practice & Continuous Learning

The coach is also continually developing.

Reflect before, during and after coaching. Learn from patterns in your practice, seek supervision or mentor coaching when useful, and stay open to feedback, new evidence and better ways of serving clients.

Ongoing learning and reflective practice are explicit parts of ICF Competency 2; EMCC also treats reflection and supervision as important professional-development practices.
MINDSET IN ACTION

What changes in the conversation?

The difference is often visible in small language choices. These examples are illustrations, not scripts.

Advice → Exploration
Instead of: “Have you tried speaking to your manager?”
Coach: “What possibilities are you seeing?”
Interpretation → Client meaning
Instead of: “I think the real problem is confidence.”
Coach: “What feels most important about this to you?”
Fixing → Curiosity
Instead of: “You need to set better boundaries.”
Coach: “What boundary would be meaningful to you here?”
Filling silence → Holding space
Instead of: asking another question immediately.
Coach: allow the pause and notice what emerges.
BEFORE

What do I need to let go of so I can meet this client freshly?

DURING

Am I curious about the client, or becoming attached to my interpretation?

AFTER

What did I notice about my presence, assumptions and impact?
