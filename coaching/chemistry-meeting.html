Chemistry Meeting

A chemistry meeting is a mutual exploration: can coach and client create a relationship in which meaningful learning, growth and change are possible?

What is a chemistry meeting really about?

The handout frames the meeting as an exploration of whether coach and client can work together—not as a sales pitch or a one-sided interview.

Growth, Value & Personal ChangeIs there a meaningful reason for entering a coaching relationship?
Trust, Choice & ChangeCan the relationship create enough trust for the client to think openly and choose consciously?
Learning, Support & EnablementCan this partnership support learning without making the client dependent on the coach?

What is the client sensing?

The handout describes several tensions a client may be exploring at the same time. They are useful as a coach self-check—not as a scorecard.

Credibility ↔ Humility

Capable without a pedestal

Can the coach bring credibility while still meeting the client on equal footing?

Experience ↔ Inspiration

Experience that serves the client

Does the coach's experience support possibility—or crowd out the client's own thinking?

Trust ↔ Challenge

Safe enough to be challenged

Can the client speak openly and also trust the coach to challenge assumptions when useful?

Learning ↔ Empathy

Learning with context

Can the coach understand the client's journey rather than simply teach techniques?

Authenticity ↔ Courage

Genuine presence with boundaries

Does the coach show up authentically and have the courage to hold values, boundaries and difficult conversations?

Build rapport before explaining the process

The handout suggests beginning warmly, noticing the client's world, thanking them for the conversation and creating space for mutual introduction.

Start with them

“I'd love to know a little about you and what brings you to this conversation.”

Invite reciprocity

“What would you like to know about me?”

Ask permission

“Would it be useful if I briefly shared my background and how I work?”

Previous coaching

“Have you worked with a coach before? What worked well, and what felt missing?”

Practical cue from the handout: explain the role of the coach before going deep into the process.

Explore readiness—not “score coachability”

The handout names several signs of readiness for a coaching journey. Use them as conversation signals, not as a diagnostic test.

Desire for growthIs there genuine ambition or creative dissatisfaction that the client wants to explore?
Openness & curiosityIs the client willing to consider perspectives beyond their current interpretation?
Learning orientationIs there interest in learning, reflecting and reinventing rather than only seeking answers?
Relationship with feedbackCan feedback be explored as information that may increase self-awareness?
Willingness to be realCan fears, assumptions, uncertainty or vulnerability eventually enter the conversation?
Follow-throughIs the client willing to turn insight into experiments, choices or changed behaviour?

Coach questions during the chemistry conversation

  • What is motivating you to consider coaching now?
  • What would you like to be different because of this journey?
  • What kind of support or challenge helps you think best?
  • What would make this relationship useful enough to continue?

What role might I play as your coach?

The handout uses three simple metaphors. They make the coaching role easier to explain without relying on jargon.

🔊

Soundboard

Listen non-judgmentally to the client's journey, ideas, beliefs and assumptions so the client can hear their own thinking more clearly.

🧠

Thinking Partner

Ask before telling. Think alongside the client, invite reflection and shape possibilities without becoming prescriptive.

🪞

Mirror

Reflect what you notice. In stakeholder-based leadership coaching, this may also include bringing agreed stakeholder feedback back to the client.

Check the fit: “Does that way of working fit what you need from a coach? Is there anything else you would want from me in this relationship?”

How might the work unfold?

The handout describes two complementary routes. These are especially relevant to leadership coaching where stakeholder feedback is part of the engagement.

Inside-Out

Start with self-perception

  • Reflection tools
  • Beliefs and assumptions
  • Blind spots and possibilities
  • How the client sees themselves
  • Client-generated awareness and goals
Outside-In

Bring in observable context

  • Stakeholder observations and perceptions
  • Behavioural patterns others experience
  • Feedback gathered with agreement
  • Goal discovery informed by context
  • Later feedback on visible change
Important: stakeholder feedback is an optional, agreed process—not a default requirement for every coaching relationship. The client still owns what they choose to work on.

If AI will be part of the coaching journey

Do not wait until after contracting to mention it. If AI, transcription or digital analysis may touch coaching information, make that visible before the client agrees to proceed.

Where?

Explain where AI might be used: preparation, transcription, reflection prompts, pattern analysis, role-play, nudges or progress tracking.

What data?

Clarify what client information may be entered, captured or analysed—and what will never be shared with the tool.

Why?

State the purpose. AI use should have a clear coaching benefit rather than being used simply because the technology is available.

How are outputs treated?

Frame generated patterns or summaries as prompts and hypotheses for exploration, not facts or judgments about the client.

What choice does the client have?

Explain consent, alternatives and whether the client can opt out of AI-supported elements without losing access to the human coaching relationship.

Who remains accountable?

The coach remains responsible for professional judgment, boundaries, confidentiality and how technology is integrated into the relationship.

Six topics to cover before you close

Use this as a lightweight checklist so the conversation stays human but nothing important is missed.

1. What is CoachingClarify what coaching is and how it differs from mentoring, consulting, teaching or therapy.
2. The Coaching RelationshipExplore how you might work together and what the client wants from the partnership.
3. ConfidentialityDiscuss confidentiality, its limits, sponsor/stakeholder arrangements and any agreed use of recordings, AI or digital tools.
4. Logistics & Next StepsCover practical arrangements and what happens if both decide to proceed.
5. Expectations & BoundariesMake roles, expectations, boundaries and any change of hats explicit.
6. Final Wrap-upLeave room for questions and for the potential client to decide freely whether to continue.
