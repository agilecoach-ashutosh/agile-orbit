SPEED in Systemic Team Coaching

An overview of Coacharya's SPEED framework and the Sona Comstar case. This page summarises publicly available material and does not reproduce Coacharya training assets.

Source note: Coacharya's public articles use more than one expansion of the SPEED acronym. The five-step wording below follows the 2025 Sona Comstar case article: Sharing Strengths, Presenting Future, Exploring Options, Establishing Structure, Developing Action.

Sharing StrengthsBuild connection by seeing capabilities and human stories.
Presenting FutureCo-create a compelling shared future.
Exploring OptionsGenerate routes toward the future.
Establishing StructureCreate systems and structures that support execution.
Developing ActionTranslate intent into owned action.

What the Sona Comstar case illustrates

Systemic context

The organisation was navigating rapid EV-related change, shorter product cycles and cross-functional complexity.

Emotional connection

Early work focused on people seeing colleagues beyond job roles, creating greater empathy and trust.

Constructive conflict

The case describes a shift toward surfacing disagreement rather than burying it.

Collective ownership

The coaching emphasis moved beyond individual insight toward shared action and system-level collaboration.

Use as a learning lens

  • Where is the team emotionally disconnected even when technically strong?
  • What shared future could organise effort across functions?
  • What structure is needed so insight survives after the coaching session?
  • What action is owned by the team rather than delegated to the coach?
