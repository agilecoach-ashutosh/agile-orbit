01 · USER STORY

User Story Template

Capture a user outcome, the value behind it, and observable acceptance criteria. Use the template as a conversation starter—not as a substitute for collaboration.

TEMPLATE

The Story

## [Short, outcome-focused title]

As a <real user / persona>
I want to <goal / outcome>
So that I can <benefit / value>

### Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] The system/application/service <observable behaviour>
- [ ] The system/application/service <validation or constraint>
- [ ] The system/application/service <error / edge-case behaviour>

### Notes / Context
- Business context: <why this matters>
- Dependencies: <if known>
- Out of scope: <what this story does not cover>
- Open questions: <TBD if unresolved>
EXAMPLE

Password Reset

As a customer

I want to reset my forgotten password

So that I can regain access to my account without contacting support

Acceptance Criteria

  • The system sends a password-reset email when a registered email address is submitted.
  • The system rejects an invalid or expired reset token.
  • The system prevents a reset token from being reused after a successful password change.
  • The system confirms successful password reset without exposing the user's password.
💡 TIPS

Make the story stronger

  • Start with the user. Name a real persona or stakeholder who receives the value.
  • Describe an outcome. Avoid turning “I want to” into a technical solution.
  • Keep it small. If the story needs several independent outcomes, split it.
  • Make acceptance criteria observable. Describe what the system does, permits, prevents, calculates, stores, or displays.
  • Keep process items out. Code review, testing completed, and deployment tasks are not acceptance criteria.
  • Use TBD deliberately. Surface an unknown instead of inventing a requirement.
🧠 POWERFUL QUESTIONS

Use these in refinement

  1. Who actually benefits from this?
  2. What outcome changes for that person?
  3. What problem are we solving—not what feature are we building?
  4. How would we know the story works?
  5. What is the smallest valuable slice?
  6. What could make this story fail or behave differently?
  7. What assumption are we making that we should make visible?

STORY QUALITY · INVEST

Use INVEST as a refinement lens

INVEST is a practical heuristic for improving the quality of a User Story conversation. It is not a compliance checklist or a rigid “ready” gate. When one dimension is weak, use it as a signal to clarify, slice, explore uncertainty or make a dependency visible.

BANKINGASSET MANAGEMENTCOMPLIANCE

The examples below deliberately use enterprise financial-services scenarios where regulation, controls, data and dependencies make requirement quality especially important.

I
Sanctions Screening

Independent

Can this story deliver meaningful value with minimal dependency on another story?

WHAT IT MEANS

The story can be delivered with minimal dependency on other stories.

WHY IT MATTERS

Heavy dependencies restrict prioritisation, create sequencing problems and increase delivery risk.

HOW TO IMPROVE

Slice vertically around a usable business outcome. Reduce technical sequencing and make unavoidable dependencies explicit.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“Complete sanctions screening UI after the sanctions engine integration story is finished.”

The story cannot deliver anything useful until another story is completed.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“As a Compliance Analyst, I want a newly onboarded customer to be automatically screened against the configured sanctions list so that I can identify potential sanctions matches before the account is activated.”

What improved? The slice includes the minimum integration and result needed to provide usable compliance value.
N
Portfolio Compliance

Negotiable

Does the story preserve the business need without unnecessarily prescribing the solution?

WHAT IT MEANS

The story describes the need and outcome rather than becoming a fixed implementation contract.

WHY IT MATTERS

Over-specified stories prevent the team from exploring simpler, safer or more effective solutions.

HOW TO IMPROVE

State the user need, outcome and genuine constraints. Keep implementation choices open unless they are mandatory regulatory or architectural constraints.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“The portfolio compliance report must be generated as a PDF using JasperReports, stored in S3 and emailed every day at 6 PM.”

The story mixes the business need with several predetermined implementation decisions.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“As a Portfolio Compliance Officer, I want to receive a daily record of portfolio breaches in a non-editable format so that I can review, evidence and escalate breaches before the start of the trading day.”

What improved? The user, outcome and audit constraint remain clear while the delivery team retains solution flexibility.
V
AML Transaction Monitoring

Valuable

Can we explain who benefits and what meaningful outcome changes?

WHAT IT MEANS

The story produces a meaningful outcome for a customer, business stakeholder or control function.

WHY IT MATTERS

Technical activity alone does not explain why the organisation should invest in the work.

HOW TO IMPROVE

Connect technical work to a customer, operational, risk, regulatory or business outcome.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“As a developer, I want to create an API for transaction monitoring.”

The technical output has no explicit user or business outcome.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“As an AML Investigator, I want high-risk transactions to be evaluated against configured monitoring rules within five minutes of posting so that potentially suspicious activity can be investigated before further funds movement occurs.”

What improved? The story ties the capability to an investigator, a time-sensitive control outcome and a clear reason for the investment.
E
MiFID II · Suitability

Estimable

Does the team understand the behaviour and uncertainty well enough to size it meaningfully?

WHAT IT MEANS

The team understands the scope, behaviour and uncertainty well enough to estimate the story relatively.

WHY IT MATTERS

Large estimation differences often reveal hidden assumptions, unclear rules, unknown dependencies or excessive scope.

HOW TO IMPROVE

Clarify business rules, data sources, edge cases and dependencies. Run a spike when uncertainty cannot reasonably be removed through discussion.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“Implement MiFID II suitability validation for investment products.”

One developer estimates 3 points and another 21 because product rules, customer classifications and exception scenarios are unclear.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“As an Investment Advisor, I want the order screen to block the purchase of a product whose risk rating exceeds the client's approved risk profile so that unsuitable transactions cannot be submitted without an authorised override.”

What improved? Product-risk mapping, client-risk data source and override behaviour can now be clarified explicitly, allowing the team to estimate within a reasonable range.
S
Digital KYC

Small

Is this small enough to complete, validate and learn from within the team's normal delivery cycle?

WHAT IT MEANS

The story is small enough to complete, validate and learn from within the team's normal delivery cycle.

WHY IT MATTERS

Large stories hide complexity, delay feedback and make progress difficult to inspect.

HOW TO IMPROVE

Slice by workflow step, business rule, customer segment, product type, transaction type, happy path or risk scenario.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“Build the entire digital KYC onboarding process including identity verification, sanctions screening, PEP checks, document validation, risk scoring and compliance approval.”

Multiple independently valuable behaviours and control stages are bundled into one large story.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“As a Retail Banking Customer, I want my submitted identity document to be validated for document type, expiry date and mandatory information so that an invalid document is identified before my onboarding request proceeds to compliance screening.”

What improved? One observable step of the onboarding journey is isolated while still delivering a meaningful customer and control outcome.
T
Transaction Monitoring

Testable

Can we objectively determine whether the expected behaviour works?

WHAT IT MEANS

The expected behaviour can be verified objectively using observable conditions.

WHY IT MATTERS

Without testable behaviour, different people may have different interpretations of whether the requirement is complete.

HOW TO IMPROVE

Define observable acceptance criteria using business rules, thresholds, states and expected outcomes.

⚠ NEEDS REFINEMENT

“The system should detect suspicious transactions and prevent money laundering.”

There is no objective condition for determining whether the behaviour works.
✓ STRONGER STORY

“Given a customer's cumulative outgoing transfers exceed ₹10 lakh within 24 hours and the customer is classified as high risk, when another qualifying transfer is posted, then the transaction-monitoring service creates a high-priority alert containing the customer ID, triggering transactions, rule ID and timestamp for investigator review.”

What improved? Trigger conditions, threshold, system response and evidence required by the investigator are observable and testable.
Refinement reminder: INVEST is not about forcing every story to look perfect. Use it to expose the conversation you still need to have: dependency, solution flexibility, value, uncertainty, size or testability.
02 · SAFE

SAFe Epic Template

Use an Epic Hypothesis Statement and Lean Business Case thinking to frame a significant initiative as a testable investment—not simply a large User Story.

SAFe alignment: SAFe describes an Epic as a significant solution development initiative. The Epic Hypothesis Statement captures critical information and assumptions; the Epic Owner guides the Epic through the Portfolio Kanban lifecycle, and the MVP is used to test the hypothesis with evidence.
SAFe-ALIGNED TEMPLATE

<Clear outcome-oriented name>

EPIC OWNER
<Named owner>

<What strategic theme, business objective, customer need, risk or opportunity makes this important now?>

<Who has the problem or opportunity, and what evidence do we have?>

<Primary user/customer segment(s)>

<metric> | <baseline> | <threshold> | <source>

EXAMPLE · DIGITAL CLAIMS PORTAL

From initiative to testable hypothesis

We believe a mobile-first claims portal will reduce claim-processing time and improve customer experience for customers submitting simple claims.

We will know we are moving in the right direction when the MVP shows the agreed leading indicators crossing their defined thresholds for the target customer segment.

MVP

Simple mobile claim submission for a defined claim type.

LEADING INDICATOR

Submission completion rate, processing-time trend and customer feedback.

DECISION

Use evidence to decide whether to persevere, pivot or stop further investment.

🔭 EPIC THINKING

Ask before funding the work

  • What problem or opportunity is important enough to justify an Epic?
  • What do we believe will change for customers or the business?
  • What is the riskiest assumption?
  • What is the smallest MVP that can test it?
  • Which leading indicator would tell us early that we are on the right track?
  • What evidence would cause us to pivot, persevere or stop?
⚠️ AVOID

Don't turn an Epic into a project plan

  • Don't define the entire solution before testing the core hypothesis.
  • Don't confuse an Epic with a collection of unrelated Stories.
  • Don't rely only on output measures or vanity metrics.
  • Don't treat the Lean Business Case as a one-time document.
  • Don't keep investing simply because work has already started.
