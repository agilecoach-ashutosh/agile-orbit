THE SOCIAL CONTRACT

Living team policies, not compliance checklists.

Use the agreements as the default operating model. Customize thresholds such as WIP limits, review SLAs and escalation times to your team size, product risk and support model. When the team intentionally deviates, make the exception visible. Review the agreements every 3–6 sprints or after major team/process changes, recurring defects, incidents or delivery friction.

Team Ready Checklist

  • Business problem/value is clear
  • Acceptance criteria are testable
  • Dependencies and owners are visible
  • Required design/API/data/security information is available
  • Item can be estimated with reasonable confidence
  • Major unanswered questions are resolved or converted into a spike

Common NOT-READY Signals

  • “System should work correctly” is the only acceptance criterion.
  • UX is still being designed with no agreed direction.
  • An external dependency is known but has no owner/date.
  • Major architecture uncertainty remains.
  • Required test data/access is unavailable with no plan.
  • The item combines several independently valuable outcomes.

Exception Examples

  • Urgent regulatory work
  • Production incident follow-up
  • Time-critical business requirement
  • Spike / investigation
  • External dependency that cannot be resolved before planning
TEAM MANTRAWorking agreement: We do not normally commit to work that does not meet the agreed DoR unless the team explicitly accepts the risk and records the exception.

RuleTeam Agreement
Estimation methodUse relative Story Points.
Scale1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21; use a spike when uncertainty dominates.
TechniquePlanning Poker or equivalent independent estimation before discussion.
Reference storiesUse previously completed stories as anchors.
What points representRelative complexity, effort and uncertainty.
What points do not representHours, days, individual productivity or developer performance.
Estimate ownershipThe delivery team owns the estimate collectively.
PO rolePO clarifies value, scope and intent; does not dictate the estimate.
Large item threshold13+ points is a signal to split or investigate; 21 is normally not sprint-sized.
Unknown workCreate a spike where the unknown is material to feasibility or estimation.
Re-estimationOnly when materially new information changes understanding; do not use for performance reporting.
BugsEstimate according to complexity/effort when the workflow requires an estimate.
Technical debtEstimate using the same team scale.
PairingTwo people working together does not automatically double story points.
Capacity pressureDo not inflate/deflate points merely to make work fit the forecast.

Core Agreement

Workflow StageExample WIP Limit / Policy
Selected8 for a 6-person team; enough to preserve choice without overloading.
Development3 active stories; one primary work item per person where practical.
Code Review2 items; reviewers prioritize clearing the queue.
Testing2 items; developers swarm when testing becomes the bottleneck.
Ready for PO2 items; avoid batching large piles for acceptance.
BlockedVisible and actively owned; blocked age is monitored.
General ruleFinish before starting; pull work based on priority and flow, not individual utilization.
WIP breachTeam explicitly discusses why before starting additional work.
SwarmingWhen work ages or a bottleneck emerges, team members collaborate to finish it.
ExceptionsUrgent incident, regulatory deadline or specialist work may temporarily exceed WIP with team awareness.

WIP Breach Response

  • Stop and inspect the current queue.
  • Identify the bottleneck or blocked item.
  • Swarm / review / test / unblock before pulling more work.
  • Record the reason if the WIP limit must be exceeded.
  • Review repeated breaches during retrospective and improve the system.

Code Review Checklist

  • Correctness and acceptance criteria
  • Readable naming and structure

  • Security and authorization

  • Configuration/database impact

Incident Response Flow

  • Detect → Acknowledge → Assess impact → Assign incident lead → Swarm → Mitigate → Restore → Validate → Communicate → Follow-up / learn

Sprint Interruption Rules

  • Production incidents take priority when business/customer impact warrants it.
  • Make diverted capacity visible rather than treating incident work as “free.”
  • Reassess sprint forecast with the PO when scope may be affected.
  • Use follow-up work items for systemic causes and recurring defects.
