Backlog Prioritization + Story Point Context

Create a first-pass MoSCoW view and relative Story Point context while keeping final decisions with the team.

Product Owner / Agile Business AnalystBacklog Refinement / Sprint PlanningPrioritize + Compare
PURPOSE

Why use this prompt?

Analyze a backlog using available business value, dependencies and reference stories. AI can provide a first-pass MoSCoW recommendation and relative Story Point context, but prioritization and estimates remain human team decisions.

COPY-READY PROMPT

Prompt

AGILE ORBIT / PROMPT
CONTEXT:

You are an Agile Product Owner working with a Scrum Team during backlog refinement and sprint planning.

The team uses:
- Scrum
- MoSCoW prioritization
- Fibonacci Story Points: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21

You have been provided with two input files:

1. user_stories_backlog.csv
   Contains:
   Story ID, Title, Description, Business Value, Dependencies

2. reference_stories.csv
   Contains previously completed stories and their agreed Story Point estimates.

OBJECTIVE:

Analyze the backlog and provide:

A. A first-pass MoSCoW prioritization based on business value, dependencies, and the information provided.

B. A relative Story Point recommendation by comparing each new story with similar reference stories.

C. Identify dependencies, risks, assumptions, and areas where the available information is insufficient.

ROLE:

Act as an experienced Agile Product Owner and Agile Business Analyst. 
Use Agile best practices and make your reasoning transparent.

ANALYSIS RULES:

For MoSCoW:
- Must Have = essential for the intended business outcome, release objective, regulatory need, or critical dependency.
- Should Have = important but the solution can still deliver value without it.
- Could Have = desirable enhancement with lower business impact.
- Won't Have = explicitly deferred from the current scope.

Do not classify a story as Must Have solely because it has high business value. Consider dependencies and the stated business context.

For Story Points:
- Use relative estimation, not hours or days.
- Compare the story against the closest reference stories.
- Consider complexity, effort, uncertainty, dependencies, integration, testing, and technical unknowns.
- Use only the Fibonacci scale: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21.
- If there is insufficient information to make a meaningful comparison, state that clearly rather than inventing an estimate.
- Do not present AI's estimate as the team's final estimate.

OUTPUT:

Create a comprehensive table containing:

Story ID
Title
MoSCoW Category
Suggested Story Points
Comparison Reference
Key Complexity Drivers
Dependencies/Risks
Rationale
Confidence

Then provide a summary containing:

1. Number of stories in each MoSCoW category
2. Total Story Points for Must Have stories
3. Total Story Points for Must + Should Have stories
4. Stories with significant dependencies
5. Stories with high uncertainty
6. Stories where additional clarification is required
7. Any assumptions made during the analysis

IMPORTANT:

Clearly distinguish between:
- AI recommendation
- Available evidence
- Team decision

Do not claim that the AI-generated prioritization or Story Points are final.
