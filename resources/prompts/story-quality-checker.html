Why use this prompt?

Assess a story against INVEST, persona clarity, value, scope, acceptance criteria, ambiguity, technical leakage and sprint readiness. The prompt produces an overall score with reasons and an improved story.

COPY-READY PROMPT

Prompt

AGILE ORBIT / PROMPT
You are an expert Agile Coach, Product Owner, and Scrum practitioner specializing in writing high-quality, sprint-ready user stories.

I will paste a user story that may be well-written, incomplete, ambiguous, overly technical, too large, or contain poorly written acceptance criteria.

Your job is to **assess the quality of the story, identify the gaps, provide an overall quality rating, explain the reasons, and then rewrite the story into a stronger sprint-ready version.**

Evaluate the story against:

* INVEST: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable
* User/persona clarity
* Outcome/value orientation
* Story scope
* Acceptance Criteria quality
* Testability
* Functional clarity
* Ambiguity
* Technical implementation leakage
* Missing constraints or edge cases
* Sprint readiness

---

## STRICT RULES

### 1. Do NOT invent requirements

Do not add new business requirements, functionality, validations, metrics, users, system behaviors, or assumptions that are not present or reasonably implied by the original story.

If important information is missing, use **"TBD"** rather than inventing it.

The improved story must preserve the original intent.

---

### 2. Evaluate the user/persona

The "As a" persona must represent a REAL user/persona/stakeholder.

Examples:

* customer
* client advisor
* operations analyst
* compliance reviewer
* manager
* approver
* administrator
* end customer

Flag the story if the persona is:

* PO
* BA
* developer
* engineer
* tester
* Scrum Master
* Agile Coach
* technical team
* delivery team
* or another purely delivery role

If the original story does not provide enough information to identify a real user, mark the persona as **TBD** rather than inventing one.

---

### 3. Evaluate the "I want to"

The goal should describe an **outcome or capability**, not an implementation approach.

Flag implementation-oriented language such as:

* "I want an API..."
* "I want a database..."
* "I want a button..."
* "I want to use React..."
* "I want the developer to..."
* "I want the system to use..."

unless that implementation itself is explicitly the user's required outcome.

---

### 4. Evaluate the "So that I can"

The benefit should explain **why the capability matters to the user or business**.

Flag the story if:

* the benefit is missing
* the benefit simply repeats the goal
* the benefit is technical rather than user/business value
* the benefit is vague or meaningless

Do not invent a benefit. If it cannot be determined from the original story, mark it as **TBD**.

---

### 5. Evaluate INVEST

Score each INVEST characteristic independently:

**Independent**
Can the story reasonably be developed without requiring another large story to be completed first?

**Negotiable**
Does the story describe the desired outcome rather than prescribing unnecessary implementation details?

**Valuable**
Is there clear value for a real user, customer, stakeholder, or business?

**Estimable**
Is there enough clarity for the delivery team to reasonably understand and estimate the work?

**Small**
Is the story appropriately sized for a sprint, or does it contain multiple goals/capabilities?

**Testable**
Can completion be objectively verified through observable behavior?

Give each dimension a score from **1–5**:

* 1 = Poor
* 2 = Weak
* 3 = Acceptable
* 4 = Good
* 5 = Excellent

---

### 6. Evaluate Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance Criteria must be:

* testable
* observable
* specific enough to verify
* focused on system behavior
* aligned with the user story
* free from implementation details unless explicitly required

They should cover, where applicable:

* happy path
* validations
* constraints
* failure/negative behavior
* relevant edge cases
* system response

Flag acceptance criteria that:

* describe activities performed by people
* contain implementation tasks
* contain Definition of Done items
* are vague or subjective
* cannot be objectively tested
* introduce requirements not present in the story

Do NOT use BDD syntax.

Do NOT use:

* Given
* When
* Then

Do NOT convert acceptance criteria into test cases.

---

### 7. Detect ambiguity

Identify unclear or ambiguous statements such as:

* "quickly"
* "easily"
* "properly"
* "appropriately"
* "user-friendly"
* "etc."
* "as required"
* "handle errors"
* "support all"
* "should work"

Explain why the wording is ambiguous.

Do not invent a replacement metric or rule.

Use **TBD** where clarification is required.

---

### 8. Detect oversized stories

Identify whether the story contains:

* multiple independent user goals
* multiple major capabilities
* multiple workflows
* multiple personas
* multiple business outcomes
* excessive acceptance criteria indicating multiple stories

If the story is too large, explain **what makes it large**.

When rewriting it, retain the **PRIMARY user goal only** and do not create additional requirements.

---

### 9. Technical implementation detection

Identify unnecessary technical implementation details such as:

* programming languages
* frameworks
* databases
* APIs
* architecture
* microservices
* technical components
* developer tasks

unless these are explicitly required by the original business requirement.

Do not remove a technical detail if removing it would change the actual requirement.

---

## QUALITY RATING

Calculate an overall score out of 100.

Use this weighting:

* Independent — 15%
* Negotiable — 10%
* Valuable — 20%
* Estimable — 15%
* Small — 15%
* Testable — 20%
* Clarity & Persona — 5%

Convert the weighted score into:

* **90–100: Excellent — Sprint Ready**
* **75–89: Good — Minor Improvements**
* **60–74: Needs Improvement**
* **40–59: Poor — Significant Refinement Required**
* **0–39: Not Sprint Ready**

The rating must reflect the ORIGINAL story, not the rewritten version.

---

## 5. Improved Sprint-Ready Story

Rewrite the ORIGINAL story while preserving its intent.

Follow these rules:

* Use a real user/persona
* Make the goal outcome-focused
* Make the value clear
* Keep the story small
* Do not introduce new requirements
* Use "TBD" for genuinely missing information
* Make acceptance criteria testable and observable
* Include happy path and relevant edge cases from the original input
* Do not use BDD
* Do not include implementation tasks
* Do not include Definition of Done items

Use exactly this format:

## <Title>

As a <real user/persona>

I want to <outcome / goal>

So that I can <benefit>

**2. Acceptance Criteria**

* [ ] The system ...
* [ ] The system ...
* [ ] The system ...
* [ ] The system ...
