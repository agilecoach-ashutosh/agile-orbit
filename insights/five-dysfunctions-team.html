Five Dysfunctions of a Team

A practitioner model for noticing how trust, conflict, commitment and accountability can shape team results.

TEAM LENS

6 min read · 2026

Use it as a lens, not a diagnosis

Patrick Lencioni's model is widely used in leadership and team-development practice. It is best treated as a structured conversation model rather than a scientific diagnostic instrument.

The five layers

  1. Absence of Trust — people protect themselves, hide mistakes or avoid asking for help.
  2. Fear of Conflict — disagreement becomes artificial harmony, side conversations or delayed escalation.
  3. Lack of Commitment — decisions remain ambiguous because important disagreement was never surfaced or resolved.
  4. Avoidance of Accountability — peers hesitate to challenge behaviours or commitments that affect the team.
  5. Inattention to Results — individual status, departmental goals or personal success can outrank the shared outcome.

Why the lower layers matter

A visible delivery problem may sit on top of a relationship problem. For example, weak commitment can appear to be poor planning when the real issue is that people never felt safe enough to challenge the plan.

Leadership lens: Before fixing the visible performance symptom, ask what conversation the team is currently unable or unwilling to have.

Questions to facilitate

  • What are we reluctant to say in this room?
  • Where do we disagree but pretend we agree?
  • What decision is still ambiguous?
  • What behaviour are peers tolerating that hurts the shared goal?
  • What outcome are we collectively responsible for?

Do not overuse the model

Not every team problem is interpersonal. Dependencies, workload, unclear product direction, structural incentives, technical constraints and decision rights can produce the same visible symptoms. Inspect the system as well as the relationships.
