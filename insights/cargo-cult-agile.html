Cargo Cult Agile

When organisations copy the visible rituals of Agile but leave the underlying decisions, feedback loops and behaviours unchanged.

AGILE

5 min read · 2026

The pattern

A team can run Sprints, Daily Scrums, retrospectives and planning sessions and still operate with the same command-and-control assumptions it had before. The vocabulary changes. The operating system does not.

What it looks like

  • The Daily Scrum becomes a status report to a manager.
  • Velocity becomes a target rather than a planning signal.
  • Retrospectives happen every Sprint, but improvement actions disappear.
  • Teams are called self-managing while work is still assigned to individuals.
  • Stakeholders attend reviews for approval rather than learning and adaptation.
  • Plans are treated as contracts even when evidence changes.

Why it happens

Visible practices are easy to copy. Decision rights, incentives, trust, architecture, funding models and leadership behaviour are much harder to change. Organisations therefore reproduce the shape of Agile while preserving the system that produced the old behaviour.

Useful test: If you removed the Scrum vocabulary, would the way decisions are made, information flows and teams learn look materially different from before?

What to inspect instead

  • Who can change a plan when new evidence appears?
  • How quickly does customer or production feedback reach the team?
  • What behaviour do leaders reward when reality conflicts with the plan?
  • Can teams finish valuable slices without waiting across multiple silos?
  • Do improvement conversations result in observable experiments?

The shift

Do not ask whether every ceremony is being performed correctly. Ask whether the system is increasing transparency, shortening feedback, enabling adaptation and helping people deliver value with less unnecessary friction.
